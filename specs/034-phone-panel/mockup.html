wave 6b · the panel · revision 2

P1 has no width condition

6a built the overlay and deliberately touched no surface. 6b builds the frame every phone surface sits in — a bar, rows inside it, and the strip that says whether the plan holds — and it is the phase where the phone stops being a stack of cards on a page ground and becomes the same application the desktop is.

Almost all of it is already written down. patterns.md §7b draws the panel in ASCII, §7c tables the row, tokens.md §7 states every value, and design-language.html §8 draws the whole thing at a true 375 px. This document is only about the places where that is not true: where the drawing and the prose disagree about how big a target is, where the shipped code and the drawing disagree about search, where four status strips carry controls too small to press, and where a shared row component quietly makes this phase twice the size the wave table thinks it is.

Revision 2 answers those three and adds the one that matters most. Settling the target question meant multiplying out the row, and the row does not add up: tokens.md §7 states px-3 py-2.5 and “44 px tall” in the same row of the same table, and at the type sizes stated beside them that padding makes a 56 px row. Built to the letter, this phase would have taken two rows of data off a 667 px handset — which is what the review flagged from using the application, before anyone had done the arithmetic. §04.

00 · the job

One component, three findings, nine lists

M1, M2 and M3 are one sentence read three ways. Every phone surface is a bare space-y-* stack: a heading, a search input, then rows as free-standing rounded-md border border-zinc-800 bg-zinc-900 cards on the page ground. That is P1's named violation — a stack of rounded cards separated by gaps — it puts the row on the Control role's ground so a list reads as a column of inputs (M1), it does it at five values that are in neither density tier (M2), and because there is no frame there is no foot for the strip, so no phone surface states whether the plan holds (M3).

The fix is one component. components.md §5 has carried MobilePanel — gap since the phone entered this document, and since 6a it is the only gap left in it.

M1 The phone has no frame. grep -rn "PatchPanel\|PanelRail\|StatusRail" frontend/src/components/mobile/ returns 0. P1.
M2 The row is off both ladders. text-[13px], text-[11px] as a subtitle, h-9, w-9, py-1.5 — nine occurrences, all of them on a phone, every one sitting between two values that already mean something. P2 · P3.
M3 No phone surface says whether the plan holds. Four status rails exist; rendered on a phone: 0. The rental tab has a hand-built total row at bg-zinc-900 — a strip in intent, in the control colour, outside the component that already does this four times. P1 · P9.

The phase is not large in components — it is one new file and one rewritten one. It is large in call sites, and §06 is the reason: the row is shared, and changing what a row is changes every list that uses one.

6b · the same rows in a frame, under a sentence that says 3 gaps · bigger type, shorter pitch

The frame, the bar and the strip cost nothing on a small handset. Eight rows today, eight rows in the panel at 375 × 667 — because a card carries 2 px of its own border and 4 px of gap to the next one, and a row in a frame carries a 1 px hairline. Six pixels a row, eight rows, is a bar and most of a strip. On a taller phone it costs one row. §09 does both.

And the type goes up a step while that happens — 13 px over 11 px becomes 14 over 12, which is the navigation tier the phone is supposed to be taking. That is only true at the row's real height, and §04 is where the stated value and the real height turn out not to be the same thing.

Look at the dashed outlines on the left: the search field and the add button are h-9 — 36 px, in a tier that has no such value, under a rule that says 44 px is the floor. That is the first of the three questions, and it is §03.

height The panel fills the body and the rows scroll inside it, so the bar and the strip are both always on screen. A panel that scrolls with the page takes its strip below the fold, and a strip you have to scroll to is a strip nobody reads.

Settled here, not asked

MobilePanel takes a bar, not a bar's contents. title, search / onSearch, actions, strip, children. The panel owns the shape — the order, the wrap, the 44 px target, the fact that primary is last — and actions is a node so that 6c can push the Rig button, Print and the rental exports in without the component growing a prop per surface. That is PatchPanel's shape with the two things every phone bar has carved out, and it is the split that keeps nine bars from drifting while still admitting the six things only one surface needs.

Search, open · the bar's second row, only while searching

why A field that is always open costs 44 px on every screen to serve a thing done on some of them. An icon costs 44 px in a row that already exists, and one tap.
and It is what makes the bar's stated order survive 6c. The audio bar has to hold a Sheet | Flow toggle, a Rig button and Print before this wave ends. With a field in it, none of those fit at 375 px; with a target, they queue.
but Search behind a tap is search you might not know is there. The counter is that the icon is in the bar at all times — it is not in an overflow — and the phone's search does what the desktop's does: it filters, and §7b already says a search box that filters nothing is absent.
state Opening focuses the field; Escape, the ✕, or clearing the text closes the row. A non-empty query keeps the row open — the filter never applies invisibly.

F3 · new The stated padding cannot produce the stated height. tokens.md §7's phone table has one row for the row:

| Row | px-3 py-2.5, two lines | title text-sm, subtitle text-xs — 44 px tall |

Those three claims are not compatible. Tailwind's text-sm carries a 20 px line box and text-xs a 16 px one; py-2.5 is 20 px more. 20 + 20 + 16 = 56. A row built to the letter of that line is a quarter taller than the one shipping today, and on a 667 px handset it takes two rows of data off the screen — 8 becomes 6.

The drawn specimen has never done this. §8's .m-row is min-height: 44px with 5 px of padding and a 1.3 line box, and the code shipping today reaches the same 44 px from the other direction, with py-1.5 and leading-tight under smaller type. Two independent implementations agree with each other and disagree with the table. The table is what is wrong.

The correction: the row states a height, not a padding. min-h-[44px] with leading-tight on both lines, and py-1.5 as what falls out of it — 12 + 17.5 + 15 = 44.5. This is the same move tokens.md already makes for the nav and the header, where the height is stated and everything else derives, and for the same reason: a padding is an input, a row height is the thing anyone actually cares about, and stating both invites them to disagree — which, for five waves, they have.

So M2's list loses one entry. py-1.5 was never the defect: it is what makes 44 px with two tight lines, and it is the value the corrected rule arrives back at. The defect was always the two type sizes it was holding — text-[13px] and text-[11px], which are in neither tier. The row's type goes up; its box does not.

The trailing slot, and the one thing it must not become

§7c: one state or one affordance, never both. A channel row shows the pencil; an event row shows its role badge; a rental line shows over stock. Today the pencil is rendered !readOnly && …, which is right — it marks what the tap does, and for a viewer the tap still opens the row to read. 05 · the strip

Four rails, one phone form — and their controls do not fit

M3 is the finding this wave exists for. The strip is the sentence that says 3 gaps or over capacity or 4 over owned, and the phone is where someone is standing in the room the plan describes. Four of them are already built and already correct — PatchStatusRail, LightingStatusRail, RentalStatusRail, EquipmentStatusRail — and 6b writes no fifth one. The derivation, the tints, the three states and the words are the desktop's, unchanged, because P9 says a verdict does not change with a viewport.

Two things do change, and one of them is a question.

The size is settled — components.md §5 already states the rails take the phone's text-xs, and the mechanism follows 6a's precedent exactly: the rail branches inside itself on useIsMobile(), the way ui/Drawer does, so all four acquire a phone form without any of the five tabs that render them being touched for it.

settled · the strip is the target The controls are the open part. Every rail carries at least one — Show only gaps, Show all, Open Rig, Show only problems — drawn at h-6 px-2 text-[11px]. That is a 24 px target. It is deliberate and correct on the desktop, where the rail explicitly refuses to let a control set the height of the bar closing the panel. On a phone it is amendment 1 broken by the one control on the surface that acts on the problem the surface just reported.

07 · the edges

What this phase deliberately leaves alone

6b builds the frame and puts every existing list in it. It does not make any surface do something it does not already do — that is 6c, and keeping the line sharp is what makes a regression attributable.

M4 !isMobile as a permission stays in Equipment, Rental and Stage Plots. Those three lines are a sentence in the panel naming the reason (§7g), and writing that sentence means deciding what each surface can and cannot do on a phone. 6c.
M5 The rig cannot be declared from a phone. The three audio/lighting tabs return before their drawers render. 6a gave every drawer a phone form; mounting them is a change to the tab, not to the panel. 6c.
M9 Overview and Settings have no phone branch. Neither is a list, so neither is waiting on MobilePanel. 6c.
M11 A print from a phone is a blank page. The Print control belongs in the bar 6b is building, but the fix is rendering the print sheet in the phone branch. 6c.
M6 The canvases. A canvas is not rows: it goes in the panel's content slot with no strip, exactly as it does on the desktop. 6d, on Sheet's un-scrimmed form, which 6a built and left unwired.

08 · unfiled

Two things found while drawing this

F1 · The specimen gives every section a count; §7e says it lists sections and nothing else. design-language.html §8's switcher draws 16 ch · 3 gaps, 64 fixtures, 24 190 kr down the right of all nine, captioned “each carrying its own count.” That is not the capability badge 6a deleted — a count is real data the desktop has — but §7e's closing sentence forbids it all the same, and the shipped switcher renders no trailing at all.

The prose wins, and the specimen loses its counts. Two reasons, and the second is the real one. A count on a destination is the strip's sentence, moved into a menu, and 6b is the phase that gives the strip its home; the same fact stated in two places drifts in exactly one direction. And the phone already has a surface whose job is all nine at once — Overview's numbers band, §4b, which M9 says needs a phone form. The switcher is navigation; the verdict lives where the work is.

F2 · Four of the nine phone lists are on pages the wave table never assigned to a phase. 6c is “the nine surfaces” — the nine event sections. Dashboard, Events and Inventories are pages, and their phone lists appear in no phase at all, while holding four of the seven call sites of the row 6b rewrites.

This is 6a's finding repeating: a finding with no phase is a finding nobody has agreed to close. The resolution is the same one — 6b takes them, because they live inside the component this phase is already rewriting, and the correction is recorded in audit.md rather than in this slice's spec, because the mistake is the document's.
