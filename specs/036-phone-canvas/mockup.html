wave 6d · the canvases · revision 1 · for approval

The window is the point

6a built the overlay. 6b built the frame. 6c put the nine event sections in it. 6d is the last phase of Wave 6 and the only one that is a new capability rather than a conversion: the Flow canvas mounts on a phone, and the rule that kept it off — “a patch graph on a phone is unreadable at any density” — is reversed, on the report that the picture of where the signal goes is exactly what is wanted on site.

Almost all of it is already written down: patterns.md §7f draws the canvas in the hand, §7g says what stays a desk job, components.md §5 says the inspector is the rail’s own contents in the un-scrimmed ui/Sheet that 6a built and deliberately left unwired. This document is for the three things no document settles — and the first is the question audit.md named in advance as the reason this phase was ordered last.

01 · the question 6d was ordered last to answer

Fit-to-width or pan-and-zoom? Neither. Both, in that order.

audit.md Part 2 left one thing open for a field test: “whether a phone canvas wants the desktop’s pan-and-zoom or a fit-to-width default with zoom as the exception. Nobody has held a 60-channel patch graph on a phone yet.” It reads as a preference. It is arithmetic, and the arithmetic answers it.

The graph has a fixed logical width. InputGraphCanvas and OutputGraphCanvas both clamp their coordinate space to CANVAS_MIN_WIDTH = 820 and place the desk lane at canvasWidth − 200 − 24. That number is not a layout choice a phone can renegotiate: it is where every stored position_x in the database already is. The phone’s panel has 341 px of it — 375, less the body’s px-4 twice, less the panel’s two borders.

341 · the phone’s panel 479 more, off the right edge
the graph at 100 % — 820 logical px in a 341 px window. two and a half screens wide

So pan-and-zoom, opening at 100 % means opening onto the Stage lane and nothing else, with no way to know from the screen that there is a desk. And fit-to-width needs 341 / 820 = 0.42, which is below the zoom floor the shared control ships (ZOOM_MIN = 0.5) and renders an 11 px node title at 4.6 px. Both horns are bad, and they are bad in opposite directions.

What makes the answer easy is the second dimension, which the question never mentioned. The graph is 640 logical px tall, and the phone’s canvas region — the panel’s body, after a two-row bar and a strip — is about 300. At 42 % the graph is 345 × 269: it fits the window in both axes, whole, once.

the answer

Fit is the opening state. Pan-and-zoom is the working state. And the reset target is fit, not 100 %.

You open on the whole picture — every box, every run, unreadable as text and complete as a drawing, which is what “the picture of where the signal goes” asked for. Then you put a thumb on the part you came for. The one control that changes is what tapping the percentage does: on a desk it resets to 100 %, because at a desk 100 % is where you can read; in a hand 100 % is where you are lost, and fit is the way back.

Nothing shared moves for this. graph/ZoomControl already takes explicit onZoomIn / onZoomOut / onReset — Wave 2 added them so the stage plot could step ×1.25 over 0.05–20 while the audio canvases step ±0.1 over 0.5–1.5, and wrote down the split: the component owns the look, the caller may own the stepping. The phone is the third caller and the second stepping. The desktop’s two call sites are not touched.

Range fit → 1.0, where fit is measured from the live viewport rather than assumed, because the bar is two rows in Flow and one in Sheet. a fixed 0.42 Step ×1.4, so fit → 100 % is three taps. ±0.1, which is nine Reset fit. 100 % Pinch yes (§7f), over the same range, about the midpoint of the two fingers Pan the platform’s. The viewport is overflow: auto and the canvas is handed no gesture handlers at all, so one finger scrolls it — with momentum, both axes, unbounded. Nothing we would write is better than that, and §7g is what makes it available

02 · the bar

Flow brings two controls to a bar with 317 px, and one thing has to leave

patterns.md §7b puts Sheet │ Flow in actions by name — it changes what you are looking at — and §7f puts the ZoomControl in the bar at a 44 px target. That is five targets and a title in a bar that holds a title and three. The bar wraps to two rows before it scrolls, which is §7b’s own escape, but two rows costs the canvas 52 px of a 350 px body, so it is worth spending deliberately.

what leaves, and why it is not a size judgement

In Flow the phone has no primary action.

The ＋ on this surface adds a row — a channel, an output — and Flow has no rows. The desktop keeps it in both views because it can afford to and because the desk can immediately cable what it just added; the phone cannot cable at all (§7g), so a channel added in Flow is a channel that reaches nothing until someone is at a desk. The Sheet view is one tap away and has the control. This is the §7b rule applied, not bent: the toggle stays visible because it changes what you are looking at, and the ＋ is absent because there is nothing on this view for it to act on — which is the same reason a search box that filters nothing is absent.

03 · the design work of the slice

On a phone, search is not a highlight. It is how you aim the window.

This came out of §01 rather than into it. Once the canvas is a 341 px window over an 820 px graph, the hardest thing on the surface is not reading a box — it is finding one. Pinch to 100 %, and you are somewhere in a 2.4-screen-wide drawing with no landmark; the desk lane is off to the right and the sources are off to the left and nothing on screen says which.

The canvas already answers this and nobody noticed, because on a desk it is a nicety. Both canvases already take the shell’s search term and dim every node and row that does not match — deliberately dimming rather than hiding, because removing a node removes edges that exist, so a partly-filtered patch would read as an unpatched one (research.md R14). On a 1180 px desk that is a highlight. On a 341 px window it is a direction, and it needs one addition to become one:

the addition, and it is the whole of it

The first match scrolls itself into the viewport.

scrollIntoView({ block: 'nearest', inline: 'nearest' }) — the same call SourceRow has made since Slice 25, for the same reason, stated there in the same words: a selection you cannot see is not a selection. Type kick, and the window moves to the box holding it and dims the rest. Nothing filters, nothing hides, nothing new is derived, and the search field is the one the bar already has.

04 · the tap

The sheet that takes no scrim, wired at last

ui/Sheet has had scrim={false} since Wave 6a, built for this and left with no caller. It renders no backdrop element at all — not a transparent one, which would satisfy the sentence and lose the point, because a transparent layer still swallows every touch and would freeze the canvas the inspector is read against. So the canvas behind stays live: you can pan it with the sheet open, and the sheet is genuinely not modal.

The contents are the rail’s, unchanged. Field, Note and Legend are already width-independent — including Field’s grid-cols-[44px_minmax(0,1fr)] label-beside-control — and every field already commits on change, because components.md §3 says the rail holds no draft. So there is no footer and no Save, which is exactly what the stage plot’s sheet does since 6c. What does not travel is the w-60 shrink-0 border-l frame, because a phone has no column to give up.

one seam, and it is the only shared-code change in the slice

InspectorRail takes its header as a prop instead of as its first child.

Today both inspectors render <InspectorRail><InspectorHeader label pill onClose />…</InspectorRail>. On a phone those three things are exactly what ui/Sheet’s own header carries — and components.md §5 already anticipates it in writing: “title (a node: the inspector puts a MicroLabel and a Pill there)”. So the rail branches on useIsMobile() inside itself and returns a Sheet, and both inspectors get a phone form without either file deciding anything. That is the ui/Drawer move for the fourth time in this wave, and the fourth time it is the cheapest thing in the phase.

05 · what is withheld

One gesture, one sentence, and it does not vary with role

§7g names exactly one desk job — direct manipulation of a spatial artifact — and on this canvas that is dragging a cable from one port to another, plus moving a box to a new position. Everything an element is stays editable: which mic is on that source, which stand it is on, whether 48 V is up. “The same flow canvas that will not let you patch will let you change what is on the end of the cable that is already there.”

Withholding is done by wiring, not by a check. The canvas is handed no onPortPointerDown, no onDragStart and no onPortClick — the stage plot’s exact move — which is also what leaves the platform’s pan gesture unobstructed. !isMobile never appears in a permission expression, and the reason is in the sentence where someone can read it.

ⓘ Patching is a desk job — open this event on a computer. Tap any box to read or edit what it is.
open by default, once. closing leaves the i in the title’s line, and the choice is remembered per note — ui/InfoNote, 6c

Two words differ from the stage plot’s and both are load-bearing. “this event”, not “this plot”, because the graph belongs to the event and both audio tabs share the rig. And “any box” rather than “anything on it”, because on this canvas the ports are drawn and are not targets — telling someone to tap anything would be an invitation to press a 7 px dot.

07 · what needs an answer before the spec is written

Four questions

1 · Does the opening state answer §01?

The proposal: open at fit, reset to fit, and let 100 % be somewhere you go rather than where you land. This is the field-test question the audit deferred to this phase — open this page on the phone you would carry to a load-in, drag the first specimen, and say whether the whole graph at 42 % is worth having or is just small.

2 · Is the ＋ right to be absent in Flow?

The argument is that a channel added where it cannot be cabled is a channel that reaches nothing. The counter-argument is that you are standing next to the source you just found and the Sheet view is a tap away, so absence costs one tap and buys the zoom control its row. If you would rather keep it, the zoom moves next to it and row 2 is the search alone.

3 · Should the Outputs canvas ship in the same slice?

Everything above is drawn for Audio Inputs. The Outputs canvas is the same component family, the same rail and the same shell — the proposal is both, together, because the one thing Wave 6 has learned four times is that a phone branch built at one call site and copied to the second is how two of them end up different. It is perhaps 15 % more work and it is the difference between one implementation and two.

4 · Is the search behaviour worth putting on the desktop too?

§03’s scrollIntoView is not phone-specific — a 60-channel graph scrolls on a 1180 px panel as well, and today the desktop’s search dims a match it may have left off screen. The proposal is to make it unconditional, which is a real (small) desktop behaviour change in a phone phase, so it is asked rather than assumed. The alternative is to guard it on isMobile, which would be the first time this wave put a reach check on something that is not a composition.
