slice 31 · rental pricing by duration · revision 1

The five-day hire that costs one day

Slice 30 gave an event a length, and named what it did not do: “the order quotes a five-day hire as a one-day hire, which it did before too — what changed is that the tool now knows the duration.” This is the slice that spends it.

A day rate is not a price. Between the two sits a table the supplier publishes and a discount somebody negotiated, and PatchPlanner has held neither. The catalog holds one number per item and the order sums it, so a festival that loads in on Wednesday and loads out on Sunday is quoted at the price of a Tuesday afternoon. Everyone using this has been doing the multiplication in their head, against a table in a spreadsheet the app already imports and throws away.

00 · at a glance

Three numbers, and where each one lives

Billed daysnew
Derived from the event's own dates — never typed. Either all on-site days (load-in through load-out) or show days only (start through end), because some suppliers charge for the truck and some charge for the stage. The rule cascades: your default, adopted by a catalog when it is imported, overridable on one event. §02.
Rent factornew
The supplier's own table of days → multiplier, imported from LL.xlsx's Hyresfaktor sheet, or typed by hand, or read from a new optional sheet in the generic template. It belongs to the inventory, because it is a fact about the supplier. An event may override the resulting number when a job was priced some other way. §03, §04.
Discountnew
A percentage, typed per event, defaulting to zero. LL.xlsx has carried this in Sammanställning all along — Rabatt utrustning — and PatchPlanner has been exporting orders that leave it blank. §04.
The ledgernew
Four lines that say how the total was reached, drawn once and used in five places: the drawer, the strip, the phone sheet, the PDF and the spreadsheet. It is not a new component — it is GearSpecs rows in a GearCard. §04.
Every line stays a day rateunchanged
The factor multiplies the order, not the line. A supplier reading the PDF has to be able to check each line against their own price list, and LL.xlsx applies its factor exactly once, to the sum. Multiplication distributes; where it is applied is a question about the document, not about the arithmetic.
Nothing is blockedunchanged
A catalog with no factor table prices at ×1.00 and says so. That is the honest answer for a supplier who does not work this way, and it is what every existing catalog means today.

02 · billed days

Which days you pay for

An event has four dates. Two of them are the show and two of them are the truck, and which pair the supplier charges for is a term of business, not a fact about the event. Some rental houses charge from load-in to load-out; some charge only for the days the event covers. Both are ordinary.

So it is a setting — and it is a setting in three places, each one a copy rather than a reference, which is the same shape Slice 17 gave event settings and Slice 22 gave hand-entered items. Changing your default never reaches back into a catalog, and changing a catalog never reaches into an event that already departed from it.

03 · the rate card

The factor table, and the catalog that owns it

The table belongs to the inventory — it is the supplier's price curve, arriving with their price list and changing when theirs does. It reaches the catalog three ways: an LL.xlsx import reads the Hyresfaktor sheet, a generic import reads a new optional Rent factor sheet, and a person types rows into the drawer.

It is read as a rate card, not as a curve. Seventeen days gets the 14-day factor — the last rate you qualified for — and anything past the final row holds at the final row's factor. Below the first row, the first row. This was settled with the user against the alternative of interpolating between rows: a rate card is a step function everywhere else in the trade, and a number the supplier never published is a number nobody can check.

5 days×2.20  exact row
17 days×4.00  the 14-day row
45 days×6.60  the 30-day row, held
1 day×1.00  exact row
no table at all×1.00  and the drawer says so
the lookup — step down, clamp at both ends. One pure function in lib/ on the front end and one in domain/ on the back, tested against these five cases and the empty table.

07 · decisions

What this mockup decides, and what it asks

Step down, clamp both endssettled
Agreed with you before drawing. 17 days → the 14-day factor; past the last row the last row holds. The alternative, interpolating, was rejected: a supplier cannot be asked to honour a rate they never published.
An import replaces the tablesettled
Agreed with you before drawing. The table is one curve, not a set of independent rows, so is_manual has no meaning here. An import carrying no factor sheet leaves the table alone — which is what makes the blank generic template harmless.
User → catalog → eventsettled
Your instruction, drawn in §02. The catalog copies your default at import; the event references the catalog until it disagrees.
The factor is not per linedecided here
Every line keeps its day rate, on screen and in all three exports; the factor and discount act once, on the sum. It is what LL.xlsx does, and it keeps each line checkable against the supplier's published list.
Two words on the old totaldecided here
total_ex_vat becomes subtotal_ex_vat in the API and per day on paper, and the priced total is a new field beside it. The rename is deliberate: it makes the compiler visit every screen that shows money, so none can keep quietly showing the day rate.
No VAT linedecided here
LL.xlsx carries an ink. column at ×1.25 throughout. Nothing in PatchPlanner has ever shown VAT and adding it is a separate argument about a rate that is not ours to assume. Everything stays ex VAT, labelled as it already is.
No new design rulechecked
Drawer, GearList, StatusStrip, Select, Input, page tabs — every construction here is already in design/, and the ledger is GearSpecs rows in a GearCard. Nothing in design/ needs editing, which is what keeps this a slice about money rather than about the language.

Two questions for you

1 · The amber words on the strip. When a catalog has no rate card, a five-day hire prices at ×1.00 — which is exactly the bug this slice exists to end, and it would be silent. §04's second strip says ×1.00 no rate card in amber text while leaving the strip's tint to the stock verdict. Is that the right weight, or should it be plain grey like every other derivation step?

2 · Owned gear. Gear you own is not hired, so no factor and no discount touch it, and the Equipment tab is untouched by this slice. Confirming that is deliberate: if you ever price your own gear per day to a client, that is a different slice.
