wave 6c · the nine surfaces · revision 1

“Nobody built it” is not “you may not”

6a built the overlay and touched no surface. 6b built the frame — bar, rows, strip — and put nine lists in it. 6c is the last conversion phase: the nine event sections go in the frame, the three that use a viewport as a permission stop doing it, the two that have no phone form at all get one, and the phone stops being a subset of the application.

Four findings close — M4, M5, M9, M11 — and Wave 6 is left with one phase and one finding, which is the Flow canvas.

Most of this is already written down. patterns.md §7b draws the panel, §7d gives the sheet its three jobs, §7g says what stays a desk job and in what words. This document is for the two places where it is not. Overview is Wave 4's best desktop surface and its composition — five 26 px figures side by side, a rig drawn as one row of a canvas — is the exact composition a 375 px screen cannot hold. That is §01, and it is most of what follows.

And three things turned up that no finding had named. There is a fifth status rail; the bar's overflow was specified in 6b and never built, and 6c is the phase that needs it; and a constant is still named after something 6a deleted.

00 · the job

Nine sections, four findings, and three the document did not have

The nine sections are the phone's copy of the desktop tab strip: Overview, Audio Inputs, Audio Outputs, Lighting Rig, Stage Plots, Signal Flow, Equipment, Rental Order, Settings. Six of them have a phone branch today; three of those six say you may not when the truth is nobody built it; two of them have no phone branch at all and render desktop markup at 375 px.

M4 A viewport is &&-ed into a role. const editable = !readOnly && !isMobile on Equipment and Rental, editingAllowed on Stage Plots. Downstream every check reads you may not. The sharper half is one line in MobileEntityList: onClick={readOnly ? undefined : …} — correct on six desktop sheets, wrong here, because a phone row is a truncation and a viewer who cannot tap cannot read the patch at all. P7 · P9.
M5 The rig cannot be declared from a phone. All three audio and lighting tabs return their phone branch before RigDrawer, BusesDrawer and UniversesDrawer are rendered — so a stagebox that arrives at load-in cannot be entered from the loading bay. 6b saw this coming and parked it: StatusStrip has a prop called desktopExtras whose doc comment says “Open Rig, whose drawer is not mounted on a phone (M5)”. P9.
M9 Two tabs have no phone branch at all. grep -c isMobile OverviewTab.tsx → 0; SettingsTab.tsx → 0. Both render desktop markup at 375 px: Overview's five-figure band wraps into a ragged stack and its rig ribbon overflows the viewport; Settings renders a two-column panel-and-rail whose rail is a column, which is the one thing a phone does not have. P9.
M11 A print from a phone is a blank page. Audio Inputs, Audio Outputs and Lighting render their print sheet only in the desktop return; the phone branch is <div className="print:hidden"> and nothing else. Patterns §7h.

And three this document adds, in the order they were found:

F1 There is a fifth status rail. 6b converted four — PatchStatusRail, LightingStatusRail, RentalStatusRail, EquipmentStatusRail — into the shared StatusStrip shell, and both audit.md and components.md §5 say four. SignalFlowStatusRail hand-builds the same tinted border-t div, with its own copy of the tint expression and its own size="xs" filter button. It was missed because it is the one rail whose surface was not one of 6b's nine lists — Signal Flow's phone branch is a space-y-1.5 stack of cards, which is M1 still standing on the one surface nobody counted.
F2 The bar's overflow was specified and never built. patterns.md §7b: “What does not fit at 375 px goes into the overflow the same way the nav's fourth slot works; it does not silently vanish.” Nothing implements it, because in 6b nothing overflowed. In 6c five surfaces acquire controls — Rig, Buses, Universes, Print, three rental exports — and Audio Inputs' bar would carry five 44 px targets and a title in 349 px. §03.
F3 A constant is named after something that was deleted. MOBILE_SECTION_CAPABILITIES carries no capability since 6a removed the badge (M7); its own comment explains that the name was kept deliberately, so a rename would not be mixed into a deletion. That reason has expired — this slice touches the file for a different reason, and the deletion is four commits old and readable on its own.

And one thing 6b built, reverted, and handed here by name. A panel that fills the body — scrolling only its rows, so the bar and the strip stay on screen — is what a strip is for, and it needs the shell to be a fixed-height column. 6b took it out again in the same slice because a column makes every child a flex item and a flex item whose root is a scroll container has an automatic minimum size of zero, so the three phone surfaces that were not panels yet were compressed below their content and clipped. Those three surfaces are Overview, event Settings and /my-defaults — and §01 and §02 are what make them panels. §07 collects the shell.

01 · overview

The best desktop surface in the app, and the one composition a phone cannot have

Overview is what Wave 4 built to answer how is this show doing: five figures set in 26 px mono side by side, the rig drawn as the flow canvas one row high, and every work panel's own verdict collected onto one line. Every part of that is a read across — five things at one glance, six nodes in one chain, five verdicts in one row. A 375 px screen has no across.

So the question is not how to shrink it. It is which of the three bands is a list, which is a drawing, and which is a figure — because the phone has a good answer for each of those and a bad answer for “all three, narrower”.

The three bands, decided separately

The figures stay figures. tokens.md §7 is explicit — density on a phone comes from stacking, never from shrinking — so 26 px mono stays 26 px mono and the band becomes a two-column grid. The tiles carry no border, no radius and no ground of their own, only the same hairlines the rows below them use, which is M1's lesson applied to something that is not a row: five cards inside a frame is the failure the frame was built to end.

The countdown spans both columns, and it is the one place this composition takes a position rather than following a rule. Four of the five figures are quantities of things in the plan; days to load-in is the only one about now, it is the only one whose tab is Overview itself, and on the surface someone opens standing in the venue it is the headline. Spanning is how a grid says this one is a different kind of fact without inventing a colour or a weight to say it with. It also disposes of the arithmetic problem honestly: five things do not fit a two-column grid, and the choice is between one empty cell and one spanning cell.

The ribbon stays a drawing, and it pans. The rig ribbon is the flow canvas one row high, and patterns.md §7f already settled what a drawing does on a phone: nothing on it is re-sized, because nothing on it is a target. So the nodes keep their geometry and the ribbon scrolls horizontally inside its own row — which is panning a canvas, on the one canvas that is one row tall. The DMX meter comes out of the ribbon's trailing slot and sits under it, because a meter beside a scrolling track is a meter that scrolls away.

And the readiness row becomes the phone's index of the show. This is the part worth arguing for. On the desktop, ReadinessRow is five area + Pill pairs on one line, each one opening its tab — a secondary convenience, because the tab strip is right there. On a phone the tab strip is behind a tap in the section switcher, and five 44 px rows that both state the verdict and route to it are the single most useful object on the device. The leading edge takes the verdict's own tone, so a run of green with one red edge reads from the edge of vision before a word of it is read.

The middle column is what the desktop figures already say about that area (16 ch in · 8 out), so the row is not a pill floating beside a label. Nothing is derived twice: lib/eventSummary already computes both halves, and this composition just stops drawing them in two places.

Two smaller decisions

The event's name, venue and dates do not appear in the panel. On the desktop the Card's header carries them because the page has nowhere else to put them; on a phone EventDetail's own header already prints them above the section switcher, one row up. Repeating them would cost 48 px of a 490 px panel to say something still on screen. The Edit details pencil stays, as the panel's primaryAction, because the action has to live somewhere and the thing it edits is this section's subject.

Overview's strip is a sentence, not a control — and it is the first one that is. patterns.md §7b already allows this (“a strip with nothing to filter to draws no chevron and is not a control”) and until now every strip in the app has had something to filter to, so the rule has never been visible. Here it states the collected verdict and names the areas: filtering five rows down to two, on a list whose whole job is to be complete, would hide three destinations to save 132 px.

The inventory row and the meta footer follow the readiness run, below the fold, as a CondensedListRow and a quiet last line — the same two things the desktop card ends with, in the same order, because their order was already right.

The same component is /my-defaults, which is in the bottom nav's More sheet — so this one branch gives two phone surfaces a phone form, and both of them are on 6b's list of three that blocked the fixed-height shell.

§7 answers the rail in one line: on a phone a column becomes a tap. The rail is a selection among nine, which is exactly what the section switcher already is, so it takes that shape — a bar control naming the current vocabulary, opening a sheet listing the nine with their counts.

The picker stays in the bar rather than going in the overflow, and §03 states the rule it follows: a control that changes what you are looking at is visible, and a control that does a thing and is finished is not. The vocabulary picker is the first, in the same way the section switcher above it is.

Folding the two together — a title that is itself the picker — buys about 90 px and costs the one thing nine panels have in common, which is that the first thing in the bar is the name of the surface (patterns.md §1). A vocabulary that cannot answer still says not stored rather than showing a dash.

03 · the bar

The overflow that was specified in 6b and never built

A phone panel's bar has 317 px in it. That is 375 less the body's px-4 on both sides, less the panel's two borders, less the bar's own px-3. Everything below is measured against that number.

In 6b nothing overflowed: every bar was a title, a search target and an add button. This slice gives five surfaces controls they did not have — Rig, Buses, Universes, Print, and three separate rental exports — and the busiest of them does not fit.

The overflow is not a new idea and it does not need a decision — §7b already says what happens and where it goes. What it needs is a rule that keeps nine call sites from making nine judgements about which control is important enough to stay visible, because that judgement is exactly how a bar ends up different on every surface.

the rule

A control that changes what you are looking at stays in the bar.
A control that does a thing and is finished goes in the overflow.

It is not a size test, so it cannot drift as titles change; and it lines up with the two props that already exist. actions keeps exactly the meaning it has today — its one existing caller is the Inventories category filter, which is a selector — and a new overflow takes the rest.

stays in the bar · actions

Print— M11, §07

overflow is a typed list rather than a node — { label, icon, onClick, disabled? }[] — because the sheet it opens is a list of labelled actions and nothing else, and letting six callers each build that sheet is how two of them end up different. That is the same call ui/GearList makes, from the other side: a node where the contents are a surface's own business, a list where they are always the same shape.

One control does not earn a menu. An overflow holding exactly one action renders as that action's own 44 px target instead of a ⋯ — so Signal Flow's Print is one tap, and Audio Inputs' three are two. The rule lives inside MobilePanel, so no caller decides it.

05 · m4

A viewport is not a permission

Three tabs && a viewport into a role, and one shared component asks a viewer to read a truncation it cannot expand:

frontend/src/components/event/EquipmentTab.tsx:30 const editable = !readOnly && !isMobile frontend/src/components/event/RentalTab.tsx:39 const editable = !readOnly && !isMobile frontend/src/components/event/StagePlotTab.tsx:51 const editingAllowed = !readOnly && !isMobile frontend/src/components/mobile/MobileEntityList.tsx:75 onClick={readOnly ? undefined : () => onSelect(item.id)} // correct on six desktop sheets

patterns.md §7g is unusually specific about how this is repaired, so most of this section is applying a rule rather than choosing one:

“!isMobile never appears in a permission expression. A capability is a role question. Whether a surface has built its phone editor yet is a fact about the surface, and it belongs in the sentence above — where the user can read it.”

Which means the three lines become !readOnly, and then each surface answers the real question separately: does the thing it withholds have a phone form, or is it a desk job? §7g answers that too, and the answer is different for the three:

a form over a row · gets a phone form
Equipment lines— a quantity and a note
Rental quantities— manual audio, manual light, a note
What a plot element is— its name, its kind, its fixture
direct manipulation of a spatial artifact · a desk job
Positioning an element on a plot— a mouse's precision over a scale drawing

§7g names exactly one desk job, stated once so it stops being decided per tab: direct manipulation of a spatial artifact. Equipment and Rental are not that. Their phone branches are read-only today because Slice 21 said “a read-only lookup on mobile for every role” — a decision made in the slice that introduced the phone at all, before anything on the phone could edit — and both files still carry that sentence in a comment. This is the requirement conflict of the slice, and it is called out as a question at the end of this document.

The rest is direct: MobileEntityList's row becomes tappable for every role, and the sheet that opens is the one the role earns. MobileChannelEditSheet already keeps every value on a failed save, so a read-only sheet is the same sheet with its fields disabled and no confirm — not a second component.

07 · the shell

The column 6b built and took out again

6b's own documentation names this as 6c's, in three places, and gives the reason it failed the first time. A panel that fills the body — scrolling only its rows, so the bar and the strip stay on screen at every position — is what a strip is for, and it needs the shell to be a fixed-height column. A column makes every child a flex item, and a flex item whose root is a scroll container has an automatic minimum size of zero: the three phone surfaces that were not panels were compressed below their content and clipped, with no scrollbar anywhere.

Those three surfaces are Overview (§01), event Settings and /my-defaults (§02, one component, two surfaces). After this slice, every signed-in phone surface is a panel or a canvas — Dashboard, Events, the two Inventories surfaces and the nine sections — so the shell can become the column it wanted to be, with min-h-0 on the one child that scrolls.

today · the page scrolls
header h-14, sticky
main · px-4 py-4 grows
nav fixed, overlaid
The body reserves the nav's height as padding. The strip is at the bottom of the content, so on a 40-row list it is six screens down.
proposed · the rows scroll
header h-14, flex-none
switcher flex-none
panel bar flex-none
rows flex-1 min-h-0
strip flex-none
nav flex-none + inset
The strip is on screen at every scroll position, which is what a strip is for. The nav becomes a sibling in the column, so the body's derived bottom padding goes away and the safe-area inset stays on the nav.

The failure mode is a test, not a promise. The zero-minimum-size trap is invisible until a surface with tall content meets it, which is exactly how it survived a whole slice. So the assertion is on the shell: every scrolling child in the column carries min-h-0, and there is a render test that puts a long list in the shell and asserts the rows region is the one thing that scrolls.

Print — M11

The fix is one line per tab and it is not a design question: InputPatchSheet, the outputs sheet and LightingSheet render in the phone branch exactly as they do in the desktop return, as siblings of the print:hidden wrapper rather than inside it. Signal Flow already prints correctly — its phone branch is inside PrintSheet — and it is the only one that does, which is a decent argument that the other three were an oversight rather than a decision.

The Print action in the overflow is the second half. §7h's “every phone surface reaches the export it has” is not satisfied by a sheet that is only reachable through a browser's share menu, and the desktop has had a PrintButton in the bar since Wave 3.

10 · questions

Four things to settle before the spec is written

Q1 · Do Equipment and Rental gain editing on the phone? This is the one place two requirements conflict. Slice 21 FR-012 says both are “a read-only lookup on mobile for every role”, and both files still carry that sentence. patterns.md §7g says the opposite in a list that names them — “equipment lines, rental quantities” — and names exactly one desk job, which is neither of them. §05 draws the editing form on the assumption that §7g wins, because it is the newer decision and the more specific one. The alternative is to keep both read-only, close M4 with the §7g sentence alone (“Editing the order is a desk job”), and lose nothing else in this slice — the drawn sheets are the only work that would come out.

Q2 · Overview's countdown, spanning two columns. §01 makes days to load-in a full-width tile at 34 px, and the other four a 2×2 grid at 26 px. The alternative is five equal tiles with one empty cell, which is quieter and says nothing about which figure matters. This is the one place the composition takes a position rather than following a rule, so it is worth a look before it is built.

Q3 · A flow row wraps. §7c says both lines of a phone row truncate, and it gives the reason: a row that grows to fit its content breaks the pitch the list is read at. §06 asks for one exception — the signal chain, which is the content rather than a summary of it, and which would be worthless truncated to “Beta 91A → SB1 in 1 → Mu…”. If the exception is not wanted, the fallback is the chain on one line with the middle hops elided (“Beta 91A → … → SD10 ch 1”), which keeps the pitch and loses the routing.

Q4 · Print in the overflow. §07 adds a Print action to four surfaces. M11 strictly only requires that the print sheet render in the phone branch — a phone can reach it through the browser's own share menu. The action is one row in a sheet and it matches the desktop bar, but it is the one control in this slice that no finding asked for.
