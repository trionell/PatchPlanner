slice 39 · the trusses overlay · revision 1 · for approval, before the spec

The plot is the work. The truss list is the bench beside it.

You assemble a truss out of pieces, hang a fixture on it, go back to the canvas, come back and move the fixture 40 cm. Nothing is waiting on you while that happens — which is the whole of the test components.md §ui/Dialog now states, and it sends this surface to Drawer. This slice applies that rule. It does not amend it and it adds nothing to design/.

The move is worth more than an anchor. A Drawer is a bottom Sheet below 768 px, so the surface acquires a phone form for free — and the phone form is the interesting half, because it edits nothing. You cannot move a truss on a phone, so building one there buys nothing. What the phone gets is the see more case: the pieces, the total length, the hang height and which fixture hangs at which offset, on which lane. The canvas cannot show any of it.

Two things change shape on the way. The read view stops being the edit view with its inputs greyed out — six disabled props leave this file and values are stated as values. And two destructive controls get an answer each, decided separately: one gets a confirm, one gets a label and a target it does not have today.

01 · the shape

A modal that takes the centre for twenty minutes is not interrupting you

components.md §ui/Dialog · written by Slice 38 · applied here

A Dialog interrupts. One choice has to be made before what you were doing can continue, so it takes the centre, blocks the surface behind it, and leaves as soon as it is answered.

A Drawer accompanies. It is a companion workspace beside the thing you are working on: it opens over the surface without replacing it, and you go on working in it rather than answering it.

This mockup does not restate that rule and does not extend it. Everything below is what falls out of applying it to PlotTrussManager.

Today the truss manager is a ui/Dialog at max-w-2xl, centred over the plot, holding — per truss — an inline-committing name, a hang height, a rotation and a rake, a piece picker with a length and an Add, a fixture picker with an offset and an Attach, plus a control on every piece row and every fixture row. Below all of it, a form that makes another truss. It is a workspace, and it blocks the drawing it is a workspace for.

02 · the drawer, on a desk

A truss is a record with two runs of rows inside it, not a card in a stack of cards

Width: wide. The fixture row runs four things across — a name, an offset, a lane and a detach — and the piece form runs three. At max-w-2xl (672 px) the truss name field is 190 px and a fixture name truncates at the third word. wide is max-w-3xl, 768 px: 96 px more, all of it going to the two names that are doing the identifying. narrow was never in it.

Structure: flush, one construction. Three answers were possible and two are wrong. A DrawerSection per truss puts a record's name into a MicroLabel heading — the label voice is a category voice (Consoles, Stageboxes), and a heading that is data is the same mistake noteId exists to stop. A GearCard per truss keeps today's boxes-inside-boxes: a bordered card holding two runs of bordered rows, inset 20 px from a drawer that is already inset. flush is the third: the drawer gives up its padding and its gap, and the trusses become one construction — a header band per truss, then its pieces, then its fixtures, full-bleed and separated by a hairline. Which is exactly what components.md §ui/Drawer says flush is for, and it holds at both widths, which is the property that matters in §05.

the truss band
Name (inline, commits on blur — unchanged), the derived total length stated in mono, the hang height as the editable number, then the state or the affordance and the delete. State or affordance, never both: a placed truss draws the Pill, an unplaced one draws Place on this plot. Today it draws a disabled button reading On plot, which is a control that cannot be pressed pretending to be a fact.
rotation and rake
A second line inside the band, present only when the truss is on this plot — because that is where the placement lives. Rotation (top) and Rotation (front) become Rotation and Rake: two labels differing by one parenthesised word is one label doing two jobs, and the plot's own Top · Front · Side already names the views. The title attributes that carried the meaning are gone; the line under them says it.
the two runs
MicroLabel + count + a rule — the app's one label voice over a run of rows (tokens.md §7). The total moved out of the heading (Pieces — total 1100 cm) and up into the band, so a number has one home.
the add rows
Full-bleed at the foot of each run, inside the construction rather than in a box: Add piece and Attach instead of two buttons both saying Add. Absent for a viewer, never disabled.
the new-truss form
Last, under its own band, where it is today. Not a DrawerSection action and not a bar button: making a truss is a field and a verb, and it belongs after the trusses it joins.
no footer
The two things a footer could say are said elsewhere — what a truss is is info, read once; the count is the subtitle, which is what “what the contents are shared by” means. And on a phone a Drawer's footer is the Sheet's footer, which §7d reserves for a confirm this drawer does not have.
03 · the explanation

Four sentences you have read once are furniture on every visit after that

PlotTrussManager.tsx:79–84 opens the body with four sentences of 14 px prose. They are correct and they are the reason anyone understands what this surface counts — and after the first read they are 96 px of the drawer, forever. That is info with a noteId, per the rule D5 restated: not the body's first paragraph, and not footer.

noteId
event-trusses. Kebab-case, after what the drawer is — and what it is, is the event's trusses, which is also the first thing the note says. The title Trusses cannot serve: a title is a label, it repeats, and since Slice 32 it is sometimes a record's name (ROADMAP D5). Namespaced by ui/Drawer to drawer:event-trusses.
four become three
The drag-and-drop sentence and the drag its marker sentence are one thing and now one sentence. What survives is the model — belongs to the event, length is the sum of the pieces — and the gesture.
and the phone reads a different third sentence
Dragging a marker along a bar is a desk job (patterns.md §7g), so telling a phone reader to do it is telling them to do something the surface withholds. The mobile mount passes its own last sentence: “Assembling a truss and hanging fixtures are desk jobs — here you can read what each one is.” That is composition, not permission: !isMobile never appears in a permission expression, and this is the same branch that already hands the plot inspector spatial={false}.
what it costs closed
Nothing. The i rides in the title's own row at both widths, and the choice is remembered per note. Open it in §02 and in §05 — both are live.
04 · the read view

A greyed-out select is not a value, it is a control apologising

readOnly already gates every control in this file and survives the move intact — a viewer keeps the read of the list, the pieces and the attached fixtures, at both widths. 05 · the same drawer, in the hand

The canvas cannot show you a truss's pieces. This is where they become legible.

ui/Drawer is ui/Sheet below 768 px, so the phone form arrives with the component and the question is only whether it edits. It does not: the plot is read-only on a phone by decision, you cannot move a truss there, and a truss you assemble but cannot place is an object with nowhere to go. What is left is the whole reason to open it — the pieces, the total length, the hang height, and which fixture hangs at which offset on which lane. None of that is on the canvas at any zoom.

flush holds at both widths, and this is where it pays: the desk's construction is already a run of full-bleed rows separated by a hairline, which is the phone's own idiom (patterns.md §7c). Nothing is re-composed. The type stays the work surface's, the geometry becomes the hand's — 44 px rows, a 44 px close — which is tokens.md §7 amendment 2 with nothing added.

06 · the way in

A count is a thing the panel says. It is not a door.

The desktop's Trusses… button lives in a toolbar the phone does not render. The mobile panel's status strip already counts 3 trusses — and that is the tempting answer and the wrong one. patterns.md §7b makes the strip a control only when it filters to exactly what it just reported, and it says a strip with nothing to filter to draws no chevron and is not a control. The plot's strip reports three different facts and can filter to none of them; hanging one drawer off one of the three makes the other two decoration that happens to be inside a button.

The rule that does answer it is the same section's split, and it has already ruled on this exact case: Open Rig, Open Buses and Open Universes are in the overflow. Opening a drawer is not a change of what you are looking at; the canvas is still there when you close it. So Trusses is an overflow entry — and because it is the only one, MobilePanel renders it as its own 44 px target rather than behind a ⋯. One action does not earn a menu, and no caller decides that.

The cost is a third row in the bar, and it is arithmetic rather than a judgement. The bar has 317 px at 375, it wraps rather than scrolls, and the plot's four targets do not fit in two rows:

07 · the destructive three

Three controls that destroy something, and they do not all deserve the same answer

One is the browser's own dialog. Two are a bare ✕ at h-3.5 with no label and no confirm. Converting all three by reflex is how a confirm becomes something people dismiss without reading, so each is decided on what it costs to be wrong.

the controlconfirmwhy
Delete truss
line 227		 ConfirmDialog It removes the truss, every piece in it and every placement on every plot in the event. Today it is a window.confirm — the right sentence in the wrong component. This slice converts this one and no others; the remaining seven are D6, and this is its worked example.
Remove piece
line 288		 ConfirmDialog It has no confirm at all today, and it is the one control here whose damage is silent and elsewhere: the truss's drawn length is the sum of its pieces, so the bar gets shorter on every plot that shows it, while you are looking at a list of four near-identical rows. Re-adding is not undo — a legacy piece cannot be re-added as one at all. The confirm names the new length, because that is the actual consequence.
Detach fixture
line 413		 no confirm The fixture stays in the rig and the picker in the same row puts it back — one tap to undo, nothing lost, nothing redrawn anywhere else. A confirm on a reversible act is what teaches people to dismiss confirms. What it does get is what it is missing: an accessible name (Detach Robe Spiikie) and an h-4 icon in a Button ghost xs — 28 px, not a 14 px glyph.

08 · the legacy piece

A state whose meaning lives in a hover has no meaning on a phone

A piece with no inventory_item_id renders an amber (legacy) whose explanation is a title attribute: “Legacy piece — not on the rental order until re-picked from the catalog.” A tooltip has no target on a touch screen (patterns.md §7g's own reasoning), and this one is carrying the only thing that makes the marker actionable: this piece is not being hired.

A drawer has room to say it. The row keeps its name and its length and gains a spec line in the warning tone — Spec tone="warning", which is amber-300 over the row's own 10 px mono, the language's existing answer for a value that needs attention (tokens.md §5). The parenthesis and the title both go.

09 · the edges

What this slice deliberately does not do

edit on a phone
Withheld, and provisional. The reason is the canvas behind it, not the width and not the role: you cannot place a truss on a phone, so assembling one there produces an object with nowhere to go. If the phone ever gets placement this reverses.
the other seven window.confirms
Untouched. D6 settles them as a set, and this slice converts only the one it opened the file for.
grouping the list
No On this plot / Not on this plot sections. A card that jumps between two groups the moment you press Place is a list that reorders under the hand; the state is on the truss's own band instead, where it is read beside the rotation it explains.
reordering pieces
Not offered, at either width. sort_order exists and nothing edits it today; the drawn length is a sum, so the order of the summands changes nothing visible. A drag handle would be a control that moves a number nobody reads.
a search over the trusses
Absent. An event has a handful of trusses; patterns.md §7b's search box that filters nothing applies to a drawer as much as to a bar.
the plot's own phone Print
Not added here. The desktop toolbar has one and the phone branch does not render it; if it is ever added the overflow holds two entries, becomes a ⋯, and Trusses moves inside it with no other change. Named, not fixed — it is not this slice's.
the desk's densities
h-8 and h-7 stay on the desk; h-3.5 does not, because §7's icon row already forbids it and the two controls that use it are exactly the two this slice is rewriting.
the rental order
Untouched. What a truss costs is Slice 31's surface; this drawer only says that a truss is counted once, and says it in the note.
design/
No edit. Every rule used here is already written — the interrupts/accompanies test, flush, the noteId, amendment 2, the overflow split, the 44 px minimum. If implementation finds a rule that is not written, that is a stop-and-ask, not a quiet addition.
10 · open

Three questions this mockup could not settle

open · for the reviewer to settle

All three are drawn as assumed, and none is settled. The spec is written from the rulings, not from the assumptions.

Q1 · Is the third bar row acceptable, or does Trusses ride in the bar instead?

§06 files the drawer-opener in overflow, which is where Open Rig, Buses and Universes already are, and the bar wraps to three rows — 52 px off a panel whose canvas is a fixed 60svh. The alternative is putting it in actions immediately after the plot picker, where it fits two rows exactly, at the cost of calling a drawer-opener a control that changes what you are looking at. Assumed: the overflow, and the third row.

Q2 · Does an unlabelled truss glyph read as trusses?

A lone overflow entry renders as its own icon target with an aria-label and no visible word. That works for Signal Flow's printer, which everyone can read. The mark drawn in §06 is a truss in elevation — two chords and their diagonals. If it does not read, the honest fix is a second overflow entry so the ⋯ sheet's labelled row does the naming, and that means finding a second action rather than inventing one. Assumed: the glyph.

Q3 · Rotation and Rake, or the two Rotation (…) labels as they are?

§02 renames them. It reads better in the drawer and much better on the phone's spec line (Rotation 0° · Rake 8° against Rotation (top) 0° · Rotation (front) 8°), and the field it renames is called tilt_deg in the database with a comment that already says rakes the truss. It is still a vocabulary change on a shipped surface. Assumed: renamed.

approval

Approving this means: PlotTrussManager becomes a Drawer at wide and flush, with info and noteId="event-trusses"; its read view states values and disabled leaves the file; the desktop trigger loses its !readOnly guard; the phone mount forces readOnly and is reached from one 44 px overflow target; deleting a truss and removing a piece each get a ConfirmDialog, detaching a fixture gets a label and a 28 px target instead; and the legacy marker becomes a warning spec line. No file in design/ changes.
