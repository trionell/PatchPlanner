Design convergence · Wave 4 · mockup for approval

Four tabs, one shape

Rental Order, Equipment and Signal Flow are the last work surfaces in the application still drawn as stacked cards on the navigation tier. Wave 3 built the template and proved it on the hardest case; these three are the same conversion with no derivation to invent. Overview is the fourth tab and the odd one out: it stops being a form altogether, and becomes the one screen that says what the whole event amounts to.

01 · where they stand

Five cards, four tables, and two forms that are always open

The four tabs between them render five Card headers, four navigation-tier ui/Tables at px-3 py-3 with text-sm and zebra striping, and two inline add-forms that are permanently expanded above the data they add to. Every editable row carries a visible Edit and a visible trash icon.

None of that is a new finding — it is design/audit.md A6 and A7, which Wave 3 closed on Lighting and deliberately left open here. What is new since Wave 3 is that the template exists: PatchPanel → SheetTable → RowEditor → status strip, and RowEditor now carries every slot these four need.

Rental order
One card, nine columns, a totals row faked inside the table body, three same-weight export buttons in the header, and a four-field manual-line form that is open whether or not you are adding a line. 50–300 rows in a real event.
Equipment
Two cards stacked with space-y-6, each with its own description, its own always-open form and its own table. The second card's rows are the same records the Rental Order tab calls manual lines.
Signal flow
Two cards, a gap count rendered as a coloured sentence above the first table, and signal chains set in proportional 14 px sans — the one thing on the surface that is a fixed-pitch string a technician reads hop by hop.
Overview
Two cards, three stat tiles at rounded-lg, and four form fields sitting open on the tab you land on. It knows more about the event than any other screen and shows almost none of it — so this one converges by changing what it is for.
02 · rental order

The tab that produces the paperwork

This is the tab with the most rows in it and the fewest editable values: almost every number on it is derived from the patch and the rig (Principle P6). The only thing a person types here is a manual line — an item the plan cannot know about, like spares.

Today that asymmetry is invisible. The manual form sits open above the table whether or not it is wanted, and each manual row grows two buttons the other rows do not have.

The editor states what it cannot change. From the plan · 8 audio · 0 lighting is a read-only field, not a disabled input: the number comes from the patch and the rig, and the only way to change it is to change those. That sentence is the whole reason this tab exists, and it is currently nowhere on the screen.

The +2 badge is the shared-address badge from Wave 3, reused unchanged, because it means the same thing: a count that qualifies the number beside it. Its title reads 8 from the plan · 2 added by hand.

03 · equipment

Two cards that are one list

Equipment is everything coming to the gig beyond the patch and the rig — that is the tab's own docstring. It renders that as two cards: Owned gear, which you bring, and Rented extras, which you hire. Each has a description, an always-open five-field form, and a table.

They are two cards because they come from two API endpoints, which is a reason the person planning the show does not have. What they are asking is what is coming with us, and have I got enough of it — one question, one list, with a column saying where each line comes from.

The Source column is the whole merge. It is a Pill, not a Badge, because this is a work surface (design/tokens.md §3) — and it is the editor's first field, where switching it swaps the item picker between your owned-gear catalog and the event's rental catalog. One editor, one switch, instead of two forms that differ in which endpoint they post to.

A rented line has two quantities and an owned line has one, because the rental order splits by department. Rather than give the sheet two mostly-empty columns, Qty carries the total and the badge carries the split — 2a, 1l, 8a·4l — with the full reading in the title. The editor still has both fields.

The hand-off is the real prize. A rented extra is a manual rental line: the same record, reached from two tabs. Today nothing on either surface says so except a sentence in a card description. Wave 3 generalised RowEditor's handoff prop exactly so a destination could be named per surface, and this is its second and third use — See on rental order → one way, See on Equipment → the other.

Sorting is owned first, then rented, then by name, so the two lists that used to be two cards are still two blocks — but on one scroll, under one set of column heads, counted by one strip.

04 · signal flow

The one sheet with nothing to edit

Every value on this tab is derived (P6): the chains are walked from input_cables and output_cables by lib/inputSignalFlow and lib/signalFlow, the same functions the flow canvases use. Nothing here is stored, so nothing here is editable — which makes it the only sheet in the application with no row editor, and that is worth saying out loud rather than leaving as an accident.

What it is for is chasing a dead line at load-in with a printed copy in one hand. So the chain has to read hop by hop, and today it is set in 14 px proportional sans: Beta 91A → XLR 10 m → SB1 · 1 → Console ch 1 where every hop is a different width and nothing lines up between rows. design/tokens.md §4 names signal chains as one of the four things mono exists for.

No Inputs | Outputs toggle, and the reason is print. A trough would be defensible — they are two views of one panel — but the tab is wrapped in PrintSheet, and a toggle prints half the document. A load-in sheet that silently omits the output patch is worse than a long one. So both stay on one scroll, divided by a labelled band, and the printed page is the whole thing.

Two tables, not one. An input row carries its bus memberships and an output row has no equivalent; forcing them into shared columns would put a dash in sixteen cells to save one heading. The band is the divider, and each table keeps the columns its own rows need. new brick — a section band inside a panel is a few lines on the inset ground with a micro-label, the same construction DrawerSection's header already is.

The gap sentence becomes the strip. 2 channels have gaps is exactly what a status strip says, tinting its ground and its rule, with a pill, the first problem named, and a filter — the treatment shipped on the audio tabs and on Lighting.

Bus chips become Pills. They are Badges today, which is the navigation tier on a surface a technician scans forty rows of. Same tint formula, same lib/vocabularyTint, one size down. This is the A9 finding, on the surfaces the audit did not list it under.

The chain is the point of the tab, so it gets the chain treatment: font-mono text-[11px] text-zinc-300, arrows at text-zinc-600 — quieter than the hops they separate — the desk's own socket in amber-300, the one hop that is not a billable cable, and a missing link in amber-400 with a shape beside it, never colour alone (P4, P5). Those are ChainCell's values, not new ones.

A row hands off rather than opening. There is nothing to edit here, but there is always somewhere to go: clicking an input row opens Audio Inputs with that channel selected, and an output row opens Audio Outputs. The band says so, once, instead of every row growing a link.

05 · overview

The tab that stops being a form

Overview is the tab you land on, and it is four text inputs and a notes box. You arrive at your own event and are shown a form for the three facts you are least likely to have forgotten — its name, its venue, its date.

Meanwhile it is the only screen positioned to answer how is this show doing, and it answers none of it. Everything needed is already derived somewhere else in the application: the patch knows its channel count and its gaps, the rig knows its fixtures and its DMX occupancy, the rental order knows its total and its over-stock lines. Eight tabs each hold one shard of the answer and no screen holds the answer.

So: editing moves into a drawer, and the tab becomes what it should have been — the event, stated.

Editing moves into a drawer, and that settles the Save button. A tab with an explicit Save was the app's one inconsistency; a drawer with one is ordinary — it opens, you change things, you save or you cancel. The read-only role falls out for free: a viewer is passed no handler, so there is no Edit details button and nothing else on the tab differs (P7).

The drawer's fields are navigation-tier, and that is not an inconsistency. The Rig, Buses and Universes drawers hold lists of many things and are dense for it; this one holds four fields. P2 decides density by asking about the surface's job, not per component — the drawer chrome is shared, the contents are sized for what they are.

The numbers band is the one place in the application where type gets big. Everything else is 10 to 14 px, on purpose, and that is exactly what makes a row of 26 px mono numerals land. It is scale contrast rather than decoration: five figures, tabular-nums, on the inset ground, each one a link to the tab that derives it. Nothing is invented for it — the channel counts, the fixture count, the picked-cable count and the rental total are all already computed and already displayed, in five different places, small.

The rig in one line is the flow canvas, one row high. Real nodes with their real border, the desk in amber-500/55 because it is the box every other box is arranged around, and runs between them — the canvas's own encoding at a glance rather than a new chart type. It answers what shape is this show in the width of a sentence: twelve sources, two stageboxes, an SD10, three amps, eight boxes. A second ribbon does the same for the rig, with the DMX meter beside it.

“Ready?” is the status strip, one level up. Every work panel in the application already closes itself with a line saying whether the plan holds; this collects those five verdicts onto the tab you land on, in the same pills, with the same tints, each one a link to the panel that raised it. It is the whole reason to open Overview rather than skip it.

What this costs: one endpoint. Overview currently fetches the event and the rental summary; the band and the ribbons want the patch and the rig as well. Fetching both whole, to show six numbers, is the wrong shape — so GET /events/{id}/summary returns the counts and the verdicts, computed where the data already is. Wave 4 was scoped as frontend-only and now is not, which is worth stating plainly rather than discovering in the plan. It is also the same endpoint the Wave 5 dashboard wants — see that page's §05. new endpoint

06 · scope

What this wave does not touch

One endpoint, and no migration. Rental, Equipment and Signal Flow need nothing from the server: every value on them is already fetched by the tab today, and the totals, counts and gap lists are already computed in the browser and merely rendered in the wrong place. Overview's numbers band and readiness row are the exception — GET /events/{id}/summary, one query, no schema change, shared with the Wave 5 dashboard.

components/ui/ is untouched, and that will be asserted by a diff rather than assumed, as it was in Wave 3. RowEditor should need nothing new either — the handoff and notice slots Wave 3 added are exactly what Rental and Equipment want.

Mobile keeps its own branch. All four tabs already render a CondensedListRow list on a phone, and Rental and Equipment are read-only there for every role. Mobile is not a third density tier (design/patterns.md §7); the phone view is out of scope for this wave.

Print is unchanged. The signal-flow print sheet stays black-on-white at 9 pt. The chain's move to mono on screen does not change ChainText, which is already mono on paper.

The exports keep their formats. Three buttons, three files, same server-side generation. This wave moves them into a bar; it does not touch what they produce.

Not done, deliberately: the rental order still cannot be filtered by department, the equipment sheet still cannot be grouped by type, and neither sheet is sortable by column. All three are conveniences, and none of them is what makes these tabs hard to read today.

07 · decided

What was asked, and what was answered

This page was published to settle four calls before the specification was written. The first three were approved as drawn. The fourth came back changed, and the change is the largest single thing in the wave — the drawings above have been revised to match, so this page and spec.md will say the same thing.

1 · Rental order — three export buttons in the bar approved

The canonical bar has one primary action and, before it, Print. This tab has three exports and no print sheet, so the exports are its print. Drawn as three xs outline buttons — Export .xlsx, Export PDF, and Export LL.xlsx only when the inventory came from a price list. The alternative is one Exports button opening a drawer that explains what each file is for. Three buttons is simpler; the drawer is the only place the LL export's unplaced-lines behaviour could be explained before you run it.

2 · Equipment — owned and rented become one sheet approved

Two cards, two forms and two tables collapse into one panel with a Source column, and the editor's first field switches which catalog the item picker reads. The alternative is keeping them as two bands in one panel, the way Signal Flow's inputs and outputs are drawn — which preserves each list's own columns but keeps two add flows. I recommend the merge: unlike inputs and outputs, these two lists answer the same question and differ only in where the gear comes from.

3 · Signal flow — one scroll, two bands, no toggle approved

Because the tab prints, and a view toggle would print half of it. Chains move to mono at 11 px, bus chips drop from Badge to Pill, the gap sentence becomes a status strip, and clicking a row opens that channel on the Audio Inputs or Outputs tab. The labelled band is the one new construction in the wave.

4 · Overview — answered by changing the question revised

Asked as “the two cards merge, and does Save event stay?”. Answered: editing moves into a drawer and the tab shows information, with a statistic worth looking at. That is a better answer than either option offered, and it dissolves the Save question rather than deciding it — a drawer with a Save is ordinary, and the tab has no form left to save.

It also changes what the wave costs. Overview was the smallest item on this page and is now the one with a server-side dependency: the numbers band and the readiness row want the patch's and the rig's counts, which the tab does not fetch, and fetching both whole for six numbers is the wrong shape. One summary endpoint, no migration, shared with the Wave 5 dashboard. §05 above is the result.
