Slice 30 · mockup for approval · revision 3

Your events are a season, not a shelf

Events is the last surface still drawn as a grid of cards, and Wave 5 left it that way on purpose: P1 exempts a list of genuinely independent objects, so the grid was not a violation. That settled the frame and never asked what the page is for. It is for finding one show among a hundred — the one in three weeks you have not patched yet, or last August’s file for the festival that just booked you again. A grid of identical boxes in creation order answers neither. A dated spine answers both.

Drawing that spine turned up something the application has quietly assumed since its first migration: an event is one day. It has one date column, the dashboard counts down to a load-in it has no record of, and a festival that runs Friday to Sunday with a Thursday rig has to be entered as Friday and remembered as four. This revision fixes that — a timeline is exactly the surface where the assumption stops being survivable. And once events have length, two of them can collide on days no calendar entry mentions, so this page has to say when they do.

00 · revision 3

What changed

Settled
All is the default view, with the toggle as drawn. The unfold stays. The season strip stays — and is rebuilt, below.
The cap is gonechanged
Revision 1 drew one bar per month, height stepped by count, capped at four. The strip is now a year track: every event drawn individually, positioned by its date, sized by its span. Nothing is aggregated, so nothing is capped.
Events have lengthnew
Three new nullable columns — load_in_date, end_date, load_out_date — around the date that has been there since migration 001. Every null derives from its neighbour, so a row with only a date is a one-day event you arrive at and leave on the day. That is every event that exists today.
Load-in is a datenew
Revision 2 asked whether a range was enough and proposed renaming days to load-in to something truthful. The answer was that load-in genuinely falls on another day, so the label stays and the data catches up to it. This also turns out to be the thing that makes clash detection correct — see §05.
Events can collidenew
Two events sharing a day are braced together in the gutter and each carries the word. Amber, as decided. Derived from the on-site span, never stored, and never blocked.
Time runs downwardchanged
Revision 1 put the future above the today rule ascending and the past below it descending, so the two nearest events sat at opposite ends of the page. The spine is now strictly chronological, oldest at the top. Reading down is moving forward. It is also what makes an event that is running right now land exactly on the rule instead of needing a special case.
Backendno longer nil
Revision 1 promised no backend change. Dates cost one migration, three fields and one ordering rule. Everything else on this page is still derived in the browser.
01 · the job

Two pages that both say “your events”

Wave 5 rebuilt the dashboard into one show and a spine of dates. It was the right shape, and it left Events with no answer to the obvious question: if the dashboard already lists my events, what is this page? Today the honest answer is “the same list, in boxes, without the good bits”.

The division is a division of tense, and it is legible from the first screenful of each page:

Dashboard
The next show, at full size. One event, its rig drawn, its countdown, its verdicts, the pulse. Then a short tail of what else is coming. You land here every working morning and the question is what am I doing next.
Eventsrebuilt
Every show you have, on a timeline. Past and future on one spine with today cutting across it, a year track that shows a whole season at a glance, search over name, venue and notes, and any row unfolding into its own rig without leaving the page. The question is where is that show — and the answer is usually a date you half-remember.

Multi-day reaches the dashboard too, and it has to. Today partitionEvents calls an event past when its date is before today, so a three-day festival disappears from the dashboard on its second morning — while you are standing in the venue. With an end date the partition gains a third bucket, running, and the hero draws the running event before the next one, counting day 2 of 3 instead of counting down to something that already started. That is one function and one label, and it is not optional: it is the bug the new column exposes.

The gutter carries every time label; the column carries content. Day number and weekday in the 84 px gutter, month headings in the same column, the year as a 26 px numeral in zinc-800 — big enough to land as you scroll past, dark enough that you never read it on purpose. It is the inverse of Overview’s numbers band, which is also big mono and is meant to be the answer. Here the big number is the ruler.

The spine is one hairline — the border-l of the content column, border-zinc-800, with a mark per event sitting on it. Not a new decoration: a dot on a line is the flow canvas’s own grammar, and P5’s reservation of circles for patchable ports is a rule about the canvas, where a dot beside a cable could be mistaken for one. There are no ports on this page.

Today cuts the spine in amber and is the page’s scroll anchor: All opens with today just under the year track, so the future is on screen and the past is one flick up. Amber is not the only carrier — the word TODAY is in the gutter beside it (P4).

The next show gets the filled dot, ringed the way a selected node is, and its countdown goes amber. It is the same event the dashboard draws at full size — one derivation, partitionEvents(), already written and already tested.

Past rows dim rather than disappear, and keep their marks. Wave 5 found the reason: last year’s file for the same festival is how this year’s patch gets built, and today that is unreachable without scrolling a grid of identical boxes. On a spine it is a place — August, one year up.

Notes leave the row. They were three of the card’s five lines and the least scannable thing on it. They stay searchable, they show in the unfold, and a row with nothing to say now takes up no room saying it.

Undated events go last. Revision 1 put them first, on the theory that an undated event is a fresh draft. On a chronological spine that is a claim that they happened before 2025. They are events not yet placed in time, so they sit after time, under their own heading, with — in the gutter.

04 · four dates

An event has a length, and you are there before it starts

The application has assumed since migration 001 that an event is one day: one date column, one countdown, one row on a list. A festival that runs Friday to Sunday and that you rig on Thursday has to be entered as Friday and remembered as four — and the remembering happens in the notes field, where nothing derives from it.

The model is four dates and one rule: each is optional, and each empty one takes its neighbour's value.

after · the ordinary case — three placeholders that say what empty means, and one bracket that collapses to a day

No “is this multi-day?” toggle. A field you leave empty is the same statement with one fewer control, and it cannot get out of step with the value it is supposed to describe. Each placeholder names its own default, so the form never has an empty box whose meaning you have to guess.

The brackets are the derivation, drawn. They are not decoration: 5 days on site and 3 show days are the two numbers everything downstream reads, and the fastest way to check you typed the dates right is to see them bracket the fields you typed. A one-day event draws one bracket, one column wide.

The mark on the spine changes shape, not colour. One day is a 7 px dot; several days is a 7 × 24 px capsule at the same x. That is P5 applied literally — the phantom-power bar exists because a red dot would have read as a port, and the same reasoning says a span must not be a differently-tinted dot. It survives greyscale, and you can see at a glance which rows on the page are evenings and which are festivals.

The capsule is the on-site span; the fill inside it is the show days. Bruksrock draws a capsule four days tall with the middle 60 % filled — you arrive a day early and leave a day late, and the row says so before you read anything. Where load-in and load-out are not separate, the capsule is filled end to end.

The gutter states the show dates and drops what it must. 18–20 over FRI–SUN. When a range crosses a month, the weekday line gives way to the months — 31–02 over JUL–AUG — because at that point which months it spans matters more than which weekdays, and 84 px holds one of them, not both. The row files under its first show day: that is the date the event is known by, and load-in is a fact about it rather than a second identity for it.

On-site gets a chip, and only when it differs. on site 17–21, quiet mono, next to the venue. A row whose load-in is its first day says nothing, because there is nothing to say.

Running now is a state, not a date. An event whose show span contains today gets the filled capsule and a day 2 of 3 pill, and its countdown reads on now. Rigging is its own answer: on the 26th, Sommarscen Borlänge reads load-in today. The pill is the carrier and amber is the emphasis, in that order (P4).

Named so it is a decision rather than an oversight, and put to you as ask 2.
05 · clashes

Two shows, one Thursday

Once events have length they can share days, and on a single-day model the app could not have told you. It should — but carefully, because a clash is a fact, not an error. You might be sending a colleague to one of them. You might be a viewer on one, watching someone else's file. Two of your events touching is information you want on the way past, and never something the interface refuses, warns about in red, or blocks on save.

The comparison is the on-site span, not the show span, and that is the whole reason load-in earns a column. Look at September on the spine: Företagsfest Ludvika is a one-evening job on the 17th and Bruksrock's first band plays on the 18th. On show dates they do not touch. On the ground you are in Avesta rigging a festival on the 17th, which is exactly the collision a date range alone cannot see — and exactly the kind you find out about by turning up.

So there are two words, because they are two different facts:

overlaps
The show spans intersect. Kulturnatt Falun runs 4–6 September and Höstsalongen is on the 5th; both are shows, on the same day.
on-site clash
The show spans do not intersect but the on-site spans do. Nothing is double-booked on paper; you are in two places anyway. This is the one the current model cannot represent at all.

The treatment is the quietest thing that survives peripheral vision: a 2 px amber brace in the gutter joining the rows that share days, and the word on each of them. Amber as decided — it is the app's “look here”; the word, because P4 forbids amber being the only carrier; the brace, because it says which rows without anyone reading anything. Red is not available for this — tokens.md reserves it for destructive actions, over capacity, a broken link and 48 V, and a clash is none of those.

It is derived, never stored (P6). One pass over the events sorted by first on-site day, grouping any whose span intersects the running maximum — the same sweep that assigns the year track its lanes, so the two cannot disagree about what collides. Which of the two words applies is then a second, cheaper test on the show spans of an already-grouped pair.

The strip counts them, in amber, at the end of the line: 2 clashes — pairs, not rows, because two events touching is one thing to look into. That is the number worth knowing before you accept the next booking, and it is the only place on the page where a count is also a warning.

What this deliberately does not do

It does not check whether you personally can be in two places at once, because the app has no model of who is working which show — only of who can edit the file. A shared event where you are a viewer braces exactly like one you own, with the role chip sitting next to the word so the reading is available at a glance. Inferring more than that would mean inventing a crew model in a slice about date columns.

06 · the year track

A year, at true scale

Revision 1 drew twelve bars, one per month, height stepped by count and capped at four. Capping was the wrong instinct: it made the strip an approximation of the list, and an approximation invites the question of what?

The track answers instead by drawing every event. One mark each, positioned at dayOfYear / 365 along the track and sized by its span, so a three-day festival is visibly three times a wedding and a busy fortnight looks like a busy fortnight. Nothing is summed, so nothing is capped and nothing is lost. The month letters below are the scale; the faint verticals are the month boundaries.

Overlaps stack into lanes. The same sweep that braces the rows packs the marks: an event that intersects one already placed goes to the lane below. A collision is therefore visible on the track as two marks at the same x — the year at a glance shows you not just how busy September is, but that two things in it are on top of each other. The band grows a lane at a time and is 15 px at two.

Today is a 1 px amber vertical through all lanes, and the current month’s letter is amber too, so the mark is never carrying the meaning alone.

It responds to the search. Type gävle and the track redraws to the matches: it becomes a picture of when you have worked for that client. That is why it sits above the list rather than in the bar — it is a readout of what is below it, not a control acting on the page. It is also a jump control: click a mark and the spine scrolls to that event.

A minimum mark of 3 px, so a one-day event in a 700 px year is still clickable — the one place the track is not to scale, and the smallest lie available. Its title gives the exact dates and name.

The year arrows step between years you have events in. ‹ is live and › is not, because 2027 holds only the undated draft — an arrow into an empty year is a promise the interface cannot keep (P7’s reasoning, applied to a stepper).

after · sheet-and-editor (patterns §2), at navigation tier, over a summary instead of a form

This is the sheet-and-editor pattern with the editor replaced by a summary. The row above takes bg-amber-500/[0.04] and loses its bottom border; the block beneath spans the content column and wears the same 2 px amber left edge a RowEditor wears; one row is open at a time. Patterns §2 already says a sheet whose every value is derived has no editor, and says so by offering a destination instead. Every value here is derived and the destination is Open the show →.

The span is the first figure. 3 days over 18–20 SEP, ahead of the channel count — because on a multi-day show it is the number that changes what the other four mean. A one-day event draws 1 day rather than hiding the figure: an absent figure would read as undeclared (patterns §5), and one day is declared.

The ribbon is RigRibbon, unchanged. Same component the dashboard hero and Overview draw, with pulse off. A node whose count is zero is omitted, so a show with no lighting has no fixtures box rather than an empty one — which means the ribbon’s shape tells you what kind of show it is before you read a number.

The numbers are Overview’s band at 19 px instead of 26. Overview is the page; this is an item in a list, and the figure has to sit under the row’s own 14 px name without outshouting it. An undefined figure is —, never 0 (patterns §5, Undeclared) — an unpatched show has not got zero channels.

The verdicts are the same words every tab’s strip uses, from eventSummary(). This is not a second opinion about the plan, and it is the part that makes the page worth opening: two of my next four shows are not addressed yet is currently a question you answer by opening four events.

The notes come back here, one line, quiet, at the end of the footer — where they are context for a decision rather than three lines of card.

What the unfold costs, and why it is affordable

Four queries — patch, rig, rental summary, plots — for one event, under the cache keys Overview and the dashboard already use, fired only when a row is opened. That is the same load the dashboard hero takes on every visit, and it is why the ribbon is not drawn on every row: twenty-eight events would be 112 requests to render a list. Wave 4 refused a GET /events/{id}/summary endpoint for the reason that still holds — a Go copy of these derivations could disagree with the tab it links to.

08 · motion

Where the fun is, and where it stops

tokens.md §6 draws the line in one sentence: a page you arrive at may move, a page you work in may not. Events is a page you arrive at, so it is allowed motion — and it gets exactly two things, both in the prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference wrapper §6 requires of anything new.

The unfoldnew
160 ms, grid-template-rows 0fr → 1fr. The block grows from the row it belongs to, which is the whole claim the pattern makes — that these two are one thing. No fade, no slide, no easing curve with a name.
Hover
transition-colors to bg-zinc-900. Already the only hover in the language. The mark does not grow and the row does not lift.
The pulseno
Stays the dashboard hero’s alone. One show pulsing is a hero; twenty-eight rows with a light running through each is a Christmas tree, and it would make the one page whose job is scan for a date the busiest surface in the app. This is the deliberate limit on “make it fun”.
Year track
No animated marks. The track redraws when the search changes, and it redraws instantly. A readout that animates is one you cannot read while typing.

10 · what it costs

One column, one page, two lib files

Migration 045new
Three nullable TEXT columns on events — load_in_date, end_date, load_out_date — with the matching .down.sql. No data migration: null already means what it needs to mean, so every existing row is correct the moment the column exists.
domain / api / dbstore
Three omitempty fields; select, insert and update carry them; create and patch reject any ordering violation of loadIn ≤ first ≤ last ≤ loadOut with a 400 naming the pair that is wrong. Handler tests for each boundary — equal dates, each pair inverted, an end without a start, a load-out without a load-in.
lib/eventTimeline.tsnew
Groups events into year → month → rows, places the today rule, formats a range for the gutter, packs the year track into lanes and finds the overlaps in the same sweep. Pure, and tested the way lib/dashboard.ts is — the page’s whole structure is this file, so it is testable without rendering anything.
lib/dashboard.ts
Extended, not copied. partitionEvents gains running and fixes the past test; formatEventDates replaces shortDate/longDate at all four call sites. Its existing tests grow the multi-day cases.
lib/eventSummary.ts
dayLabel gains running. Twenty-three tests already assert this file agrees with the tabs; the new label is asserted the same way.
pages/Events.tsx
Rewritten. Loses Card and CardContent; keeps matchesSearch, EventFormDialog and the mobile branch.
Four date call sites
Dashboard (hero + rows), EventDetail’s heading, OverviewTab and print/PrintSheet — all move onto formatEventDates. EventFormDialog and EventDetailsDrawer gain the second field.
components/ui/
No new component. The track and the spine are page-local until something else needs them; RigRibbon, Pill, Badge, MicroLabel, Button and Input are imported as they are.
design/
Same commit: patterns.md gains §4c · the spine beside §4b’s summary surface; tokens.md §6 records the unfold as the second sanctioned motion with its wrapper, and §5 records that an overlap is amber-plus-a-word and never red; audit.md records that P1’s exception for Events was invoked correctly and outlived its usefulness.
the ask

Two decisions left

1 · Does date get renamed to start_date?

With load_in_date, end_date and load_out_date beside it, the column called date is the odd one out, and a reader of the schema has to be told which of the four it is. Against renaming: it is a column rename plus a JSON field rename, touching every handler, every stored payload shape and eight call sites, to fix a cosmetic asymmetry in a slice that otherwise only adds. Recommendation: keep date, and let the four labels in the form carry the naming — Load-in, First day, Last day, Load-out — since that is where anyone actually meets them. Happy to do the rename if you would rather the schema read cleanly, but it should be its own commit.

2 · Does the rental order learn about days in this slice, or the next?

Once the app knows Bruksrock is five days on site, an order that quotes it as a one-day hire is visibly wrong rather than merely unknowing — and the unfold puts 5 and 24 190 kr on the same line, which is where you will notice. Doing it here means a per-day rate on inventory items, a duration multiplier through the aggregation, and every export format following: real work, in the one area of the app with a standing invariant about staying in sync. Recommendation: next slice, specified immediately after this one so the gap is short. Say the word if you would rather it landed together.
