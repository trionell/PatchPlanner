slice 37 · the flow canvas as a plane · revision 1 · for approval

A position that means one place

The report was that the rig lane is tighter on a phone, so the nodes bunch together and overlap. It is not tighter by a little. It is 92 pixels wide, for nodes that are 200 pixels wide — and the cause is that both flow canvases derive their coordinate space from the browser window, so a saved position means a different place on every screen it is opened on.

This is not a defect Wave 6d introduced. 6d pinned the phone’s space at 820, which made a pre-existing flaw impossible to ignore: the position columns have been recording the width of whichever window last touched them. Four different window widths are sitting in this event right now, written down as node coordinates. §01 shows them.

The fix is the model the stage plot already uses: an absolute plane, with pan and zoom as the only view state. The side rails are what makes that impossible today, so they stop being pinned — which is the change this document asks approval for.

01 · the defect, in your own data

The rig lane is 92 px on every phone

Both canvases compute the middle lane from the canvas width, and the canvas width from the panel:

zoneProcessingBounds(w) = { min: 264, max: w − NODE_WIDTH − 264 }
canvasWidth = isMobile ? 820 : max(820, panelWidth) / zoom

On a phone that second line is a constant, so the first resolves to { min: 264, max: 356 } on every handset ever made. Every stagebox, stage multi, outboard device and desk jack panel is clamped into 92 px of travel. They are 200 px wide. The lane cannot hold two of them side by side; it cannot hold one of them.

Your desk works in a space of at least 1672 — that is readable off the values it has written — where the same lane is about 1144 px. The two surfaces disagree by a factor of twelve, and the clamp runs on every node, every render.

02 · why it is the rails

Two nodes are pinned to a moving edge, and everything else pays

The Sources rail sits at x = 24 and the Channels rail at canvasWidth − 224. That second term is the whole problem. Because one node must touch the right-hand edge, the coordinate space has to know where the right-hand edge is — so the space is sized to the window, and the lane between the rails is whatever is left over. Every canvasWidth in the layout traces back to that one pin.

03 · the model

An absolute plane, with nothing pinned to anything

The stage plot has never had this problem, because its coordinates are centimetres of real floor and its view is { zoom, panX, panY } over an unbounded plane. Nothing in it is positioned relative to the window. That is the model to adopt, and adopting it deletes code rather than adding it:

the space
Unbounded, non-negative, sized to its content. Sized to the panel divided by zoom. It grows as nodes are placed further out, the way a long document’s scroll area grows.
a saved x
Means one place, on every screen. Means a fraction of whatever window is open.
the rails
Ordinary nodes at their own coordinates, dragged like any other. Pinned to the canvas’s left and right edges. They stay consolidated — one Sources node holding every source, not a card each; 32 sources as cards is 1,700 px of lane and that reasoning still holds. What goes is the pinning, not the rail.
the clamp
Deleted. clampProcessingX, on every node, every render. A node goes where it is put. The only bound is x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0, because a scroll container has no negative side.
panning
The platform’s own scrolling, on both reaches. A pointer-drag handler like the stage plot’s. The stage plot drags because it is one SVG with a viewBox; the flow canvas is real DOM with scrollable rails inside it, so native scroll is both easier and better — momentum on the phone, and it is what Wave 6d already built.
the lane bands
Gone. Three tinted regions computed from the canvas width. A band that says Rig is only true if something enforces it, and nothing will. Q1 asks whether you want them back as fixed regions instead.

What this costs on the desk

One thing, and it is worth stating plainly: the Channels rail stops hugging the right edge of the window. Today it is always there, at the edge, whatever your zoom. Under the plane it sits at a coordinate, and on a narrow window you scroll or zoom out to reach it — the same way you already reach the right-hand side of a stage plot. In exchange the rail becomes draggable, and where you put it stays put.

04 · the view, remembered

Each surface opens where you left it

With a real coordinate space, fit stops being a sensible opening state: a graph 1,600 px wide fitted into a 341 px phone is 21 %, and a 200 px node at 21 % is 42 px — narrower than a fingertip, with no legible text in it. So the canvas opens where it was last left, per event and per surface, exactly as a stage plot does.

events += input_flow_zoom, input_flow_pan_x, input_flow_pan_y,
          output_flow_zoom, output_flow_pan_x, output_flow_pan_y

Saved on an 800 ms debounce and gated on role, both copied from StagePlotTab — a viewer pans and zooms freely and writes nothing back. Pan is stored in logical units rather than scroll pixels, so it survives a zoom change and a different screen. This is what makes it a backend slice: migration 047, six columns, and the event handler.

Fit becomes a button, and starts telling the truth

Today fit is computed from a nominal 820 × 640. Under the plane it is computed from the bounding box of the nodes that actually exist, so it frames your graph rather than a rectangle somebody guessed. It stays on the zoom cluster where 6d put it, and it is how you get your bearings after panning into empty space.

The one-time tidy

§01 showed that the saved x of every pinned node is an artefact — 1086 and 1338 are two different window widths, not two placements. Adopting them would open your outputs graph with the speakers in a ragged diagonal. So migration 047 normalises them once: every node whose x was previously ignored is set to a tidy column, and from then on it means what it says. Q2 asks which column.

06 · what needs your answer

Four questions

Q1 · Do the lane bands come back as fixed regions?

Slice 25 added Stage / Rig / Console as tinted bands to say which way signal flows. Under the plane they cannot be computed any more. Three options: drop them (my recommendation — the cables already say which way signal goes, and a band nothing enforces is decoration); keep them as fixed regions at constant coordinates, honest but arbitrary once nodes may sit anywhere; or keep only the three labels across the top as a legend.

Q2 · Where does the one-time tidy put the previously-pinned nodes?

Migration 047 has to choose a column for the Channels/Destinations rail and the destination devices, because their saved values are window widths rather than placements. I propose x = 1400 for the right-hand group, leaving the left-hand group at 24 — which puts your amplifier at 743 comfortably between them, and matches roughly where you have been working. Say if you would rather it were wider or tighter.

Q3 · Desk and phone share one saved view. Is that right?

The stage plot shares zoom/pan_x/pan_y across every screen, and you have said that model works. But a flow graph genuinely wants a different zoom in a hand than on a desk, so sharing means a phone session can leave the desk opening at 40 %. The alternative is per-reach columns — twice the storage, each surface remembering its own place. I lean shared, on the precedent, and would revisit after you have used it.

Q4 · Should a node be draggable into negative space?

Native scroll has no negative side, so the plane starts at 0,0 and nothing can go above or left of it. I recommend accepting that — signal reads left to right and there is nothing to the left of the sources. It is the one real difference from the stage plot, which pans freely into negative floor.

Not in this slice

D2 stays on the roadmap: the flow inspectors still delete through window.confirm. And no node’s appearance changes here — this is the coordinate space and the view, nothing about how a box is drawn.

approval

Approving this means: the rails stop being pinned, clampProcessingX and every canvasWidth term leave the layout, each canvas opens where you left it, and the Channels rail no longer hugs the right edge of a desktop window. Answer Q1–Q4 and I will write the spec.
