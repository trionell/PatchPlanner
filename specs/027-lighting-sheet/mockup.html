Design convergence · Wave 3 · mockup for approval

Lighting becomes a sheet

The fixture table is the last surface in the application that is a form pretending to be a list. Twenty-four live controls, thirteen columns, and no read state at all — a rig of two hundred fixtures cannot be scanned, only filled in. This is what it becomes, drawn entirely in bricks the audio tabs already ship.

01 · what is there now

Thirteen columns, and every cell is a control

Below is today's Lighting tab, reproduced at its real sizes. It is a navigation-tier ui/Table — px-3 py-3 rows, text-sm, zebra striping — with an Input or a Select in almost every cell, persisting on blur.

The zebra striping is the tell. At six rows it is tidy; at two hundred it is banding. And because there is no read state, the eye has to parse a border and a background before it reaches a number, on every cell, in every row.

02 · what it becomes

One panel, nine columns, nothing to parse but the data

The same rig, as the work panel every dense tab converges on: a bar, a sheet, and a strip that closes it from inside. Clicking a row opens its editor directly beneath. Nothing in a row is a form control.

Row height goes from about 90 px to 25 px, so a screen shows roughly twenty-eight fixtures instead of seven. That is the whole argument — a rig is a thing you read down, and until now you could not.

03 · the strip

Four conditions, and only two of them are wrong

The strip is the one genuinely new capability in this wave, and it is the reason the wave is worth doing rather than a restyle. Nothing in the application today knows what the DMX map looks like. Auto-assign packs each universe from address 1 so its own output is always clean, but a single hand-edited address afterwards puts two moving heads on the same channels and the tool says nothing until a technician is standing in the room.

The difficulty is that two fixtures on the same channels is usually a mistake and sometimes the entire point. Twenty identical pars washing a room are patched to one address so one fader drives all twenty; loads on a dimmer channel are the same arrangement. A tool that called that an error would be wrong about the most ordinary thing in environment lighting, and would train the planner to ignore the strip.

Shared Identical spans — same universe, same start, same channel count.legitimate Marked in the address cell with the size of the group, counted in the strip in every state, warned about nowhere. Recognised by the addresses agreeing, not by a stored flag: a planner who patches twenty pars to one address has already said everything there is to say.
Clash Spans that intersect without being identical.error There is no lighting practice that produces a fixture on 049–063 beside one on 056–070. Same start address with a different channel count counts here too — the shorter fixture's channels being a subset of the longer one's is not what patching two fixtures together means.
Out of bounds A span running past channel 512.error Worded distinctly from a clash, because the fix is different. Its channels within the universe still count toward occupancy; the fixture is not truncated, moved or refused.
Unaddressed No start address at all.not yet done Not wrong — work outstanding. Takes no part in sharing or clash detection.

All four can hold in one universe at once, and one fixture can be both a member of a shared group and party to a clash with a third. Reporting one must never suppress another. The strip's three states are ordered by how much attention they need — errors, then unaddressed, then clean — matching how the audio strip orders over-capacity, gaps, complete.

04 · adding

One editor replaces two dialogs

Add Fixture and Bulk Add are the same form with one field's difference. They are two separate dialogs today, holding two copies of the model picker, the mode picker and the channel count — which is why the single-add dialog quietly lacks the universe and power fields the bulk one has.

+ Fixtures opens the row editor at the foot of the sheet with a Qty field defaulting to 1. Adding one fixture and adding twelve stop being different operations, and the editor you add with is the editor you edit with — which is the pattern's own rule.

05 · making room

An occupied address is a question, not a refusal

Reading the map and repacking the whole of it are the two ends of the job. What a planner actually does most often sits between them: putting one fixture at one address, in a universe that already has fixtures either side of it.

Today that is arithmetic on paper. Inserting a 16-channel head at address 49 means finding every fixture from 49 upwards and re-addressing each one by hand, in order, without slipping — while the tool that could do it in a step waits to be told the answer.

So a taken address is neither refused nor silently accepted. It is a question, asked where the address was typed, with the answers that are actually available.

Exact match. Share address appears only when the channel count matches too.
Make room Ripples, it does not bulldoze. The new span is placed, then each following group moves up only as far as it must to clear the one before it — stopping at the first group that already clears. A gap a planner left on purpose beyond the ripple survives, and nothing below the insertion point moves at all. Same invariants as the bulk operations, because it is the same packing: groups move whole, sharing survives, and nothing leaves its universe.
Share address Offered only on an exact match of address and channel count.conditional A 4-channel par at the same start as a 16-channel wash is not sharing — it is one sitting inside the other — so the option is absent and the answer is make room or place anyway.
Place anyway Always there.always A rig legitimately passes through a broken state while it is being renumbered, so no address is ever refused. The strip picks the clash up as it would any other.
Refused Only make room, and only when it cannot fit. If the ripple would push a fixture past 512 the option says so and steps aside; the other answers stay. Nothing spills into the next universe — that is the whole point of a universe being a physical boundary.
Batches The add editor gains an Addresses setting: consecutive or all share one.new Twenty pars on one address in one step, consuming seven channels rather than a hundred and forty — which is how an environment-lighting rig is actually built. Console IDs still increment; only the address is shared.
06 · the drawer

Where the universes are, and where auto-assign belongs

A universe is declared once and referenced by every fixture in it — which is the definition of a drawer, not a card stacked above the table. It is also the only place an “universe 2 exceeds 512 channels” error can be explained rather than merely reported, so both bulk operations move in here and stop competing with the primary action in the bar.

With that move the bar is exactly the canonical order with no exceptions: title, search, drawer, flex-1, print, one primary action.

Auto-assign Renumber the whole thing. Each universe repacked from address 1 in the rig's order. Unaddressed fixtures get an address. What you reach for when the numbering has stopped meaning anything.
Optimise Keep my order, close the gaps.new Each universe repacked from address 1 in the order its fixtures are already in. Unaddressed fixtures are left alone. What you reach for mid-build, when the order is right and deletions have left holes.
Both Move groups, not fixtures. Twenty pars sharing one address move as one block of seven channels and come back still sharing; two fixtures that were separate stay separate. No fixture leaves its universe — a universe is usually a cable run to one truss. A universe that will not fit is left exactly as it was and named, while the others are packed anyway.

07 · the ledger

Every column, accounted for

Thirteen columns become nine. Nothing is lost — three move into the editor, one is deleted as a duplicate of another, and one becomes readable for the first time.

08 · scope

What this wave does not touch

Mobile is unchanged. The phone view is already a list and a per-fixture edit sheet, which is the right shape; it uses navigation-tier sizing and keeps it.

Print is unchanged. The lighting rig sheet is a separate visual language and stays black-on-white at 9 pt.

One schema change, and it only removes. A migration drops the dead dmx_chain_parent_id column. Nothing is added to a fixture: every value drawn above already exists on it, and the whole DMX map — spans, sharing, clashes, gaps, occupancy — is derived in the browser from data already fetched, in the same shape as patchCapacity and testable without rendering anything.

Auto-assign is rewritten, not left alone. It moves groups instead of fixtures, so sharing survives it; it never moves a fixture between universes; and a universe that cannot fit is skipped and named rather than failing the whole rig. Optimise channels is its new sibling. This is the one place the wave changes behaviour rather than presentation, and it is deliberate — blessing a shared group in the sheet while the button beside it dissolves the group would be a trap of our own making.

Still left alone: none of the three operations can be undone, neither bulk operation reorders the rig, and neither can be run on a single universe. The first two match what auto-assign does today; the third is a convenience that costs a control per card and blocks nothing, since a universe that will not fit is skipped rather than fatal.

09 · decided

What was asked, and what was answered

This page was published to settle four calls before the specification was written. All four were approved, and three corrections came back with them; the drawings above have been revised to match, so this page and spec.md say the same thing.

1 · Deleting the DMX Chain column — approved, and taken further

Asked as “remove it from the sheet, leave the stored column alone”. Answered: delete it outright. A migration drops dmx_chain_parent_id, and it leaves the Go struct, the queries, the API and the frontend type with it. This is what makes the wave more than frontend-only.

2 · Merging Add Fixture and Bulk Add into one editor — approved

One form with Qty, defaulting to 1, at the foot of the sheet. Two dialogs and one duplicated model picker go.

3 · The Universes drawer, and auto-assign moving into it — approved

The bar keeps its canonical shape, and the 512-channel limit becomes something you can see before you hit it rather than an error afterwards. Auto-assign now has a sibling in there — see 7.

4 · Errors as an alert, never a block — approved

Flagged in the cell and counted in the strip; saving is never prevented — the same treatment duplicate console IDs already get, because a rig legitimately passes through a broken state while it is being renumbered.

5 · Sharing an address is legitimate — added on review

The first draft called every overlap an error, which would have been wrong about dimmers and environment lighting. Sharing is now recognised by identical spans rather than by a stored flag, on the grounds that a group is already fully described by its members' addresses and a second place to say so is a second thing to keep in sync. §03 above is the result.

6 · Occupancy must survive all four conditions at once — added on review

Gaps, sharing, clashes and out-of-bounds spans can hold in one universe simultaneously, and each must be reported without masking the others. That is why the drawer states the largest free run beside the free total: 494 free channels scattered in gaps of four will not take a 16-channel fixture, and the number that looks reassuring is the one that answers the wrong question.

7 · Auto-assign rewritten, and given a sibling — added on review

Recognising a shared group and then dissolving it on the next button was flagged as a trap, and the answer is to fix the button rather than warn about it. Both operations now move groups: what shared before still shares, what did not still does not. Neither moves a fixture between universes, because a universe is usually a cable run to one truss. A universe that cannot fit is skipped and named instead of failing the whole rig. And Optimise channels joins it — same packing, ordered by where fixtures already are rather than by rig order, so gaps close without the numbering being thrown away.

8 · Make room, and batches that share — added on review

An address that is already taken now asks rather than refusing or silently accepting: make room ripples the fixtures above it up by just enough, share address appears when the channel count matches exactly, and place anyway is always there because no address is ever refused. The add editor gains an Addresses setting so a whole batch can land on one address — twenty pars for seven channels, in one step. §05 above is the result.
