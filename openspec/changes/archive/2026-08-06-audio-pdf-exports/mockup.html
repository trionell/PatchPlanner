Slice 40 · design review · revision 2 · not yet approved

One column was carrying a graph.
Take it out and give the graph a page.

The audio exports stop being whatever the browser makes of a hidden React sheet and become one generated document, written by Go the way the rental order already is. Everything below is drawn at true A4 — the millimetres and points on these sheets are the numbers that go into the writer.

A4 · 12 mm margins · 186 mm text block Helvetica, cp1252 one file · five sections · two orientations
What the request actually said

Three things were wrong, and only one of them was the table

The chain column. Eight columns on the input sheet, six on the output, and in both of them one column held an entire signal path as an arrow-joined string. It squeezed every other column to do it, and it still wasn't readable. That column is gone.

The information it was hiding. A source already knows its kind, its microphone, its stand, its phantom state and its connector. None of that reached paper — a crew read it out of the chain string or not at all. The space the chain column gives up is exactly the space those fields need.

The document. window.print() cannot repeat a column header across a page break, cannot state a scale, cannot put a landscape page in the middle of a portrait one, and cannot be told how to route a line. All four are needed here, so this follows the rental order: one endpoint, one .pdf, one file that arrives whole.

The sheet a stage tech reads at load-in

1

The masthead is the document's only large type

Event name at 17 pt, venue and dates at 9 pt, provenance at 7 pt. Audio sits opposite it as a standing word, so the same block serves Slice 41's plot document with one word changed. Below it, a 0.5 mm rule — the heaviest line in the document, used exactly once.

2

Nine columns, 186 mm, and the source finally on paper

Ch 9 · Name 33 · W 6 · Source 27 · Microphone/DI 40 · Stand 23 · 48 V 9 · Conn 16 · Buses 23. Six of those nine are what the chain column was standing on top of. The widest column is the catalogue item, because that is the string a tech carries to the case.

3

A note is a second line, not a tenth column

Notes are rare and long — the two things a fixed-width column handles worst. So a row with a note grows a 6.8 pt italic line beneath it, spanning the full text block, and a row without one costs nothing. The same line states the gap on channel 19: a channel with no source says so in words rather than by nine empty cells.

4

Colour is carried, and nothing is carried only by colour

The channel strip colour returns as a 1.2 mm stripe at the row's left edge. It survives a mono laser printer as a grey bar and a colour one as itself, and no fact on the sheet is legible only through it. Phantom power is a drawn 1.6 mm square, not a bullet character — see the font rule below.

5

Legend once, footer every page

The legend prints at the end of its section, not on every page — it is read once. The footer is on all eight: document title left, page / total right, which is the rental order's footer with the centred variant dropped.

Page 4 — portrait

The section that explains the other four

6

The running head names the section, not the document

Page 1 gets the masthead; every page after it gets a 7 pt line saying which section you are inside and that it is a continuation. The column header repeats under it on every page — the one thing browser print could never be made to do, and the reason a 24-channel patch was unreadable past the fold.

7

Sections flow; they do not each claim a fresh page

Stage rig starts 8 mm below where the output patch ended. Paper is the scarce thing at a production desk, and a section break that costs half a sheet is a break nobody asked for. The only exception is the diagram, which changes orientation and therefore must start a page.

8

The rig list, and the one thing it deliberately does not claim

Box 38 · Catalogue item 46 · Kind 20 · Connection 24 · In 16 · Out 16 · Spare 26. Every figure is derived from what is already stored: installed counts from the box, patched counts from the cable graph, spare by subtraction.

There is no Location column, and there cannot be one. position_x/position_y on a stagebox is canvas placement in the flow graph, not where the box physically stands — a column headed Stage right filled from that number would be a confident lie. Adding a real field is a schema change and a different slice.

9

The summary line is the answer to the question the list provokes

Anyone reading a rig list is deciding whether one more input fits. The rule above it is 0.35 mm — the same weight as a column header, because it is a total and not a total of money. It is the only arithmetic in the document.

The rule that decides whether the diagram works at all

Lines may cross. Lines may never share a line.

Every cable on paper is the same black hairline. Two parallel runs that touch stop being two runs — a reader following one down the page has no way to know which one they are still on, and the drawing quietly lies about a patch. A crossing is different: two lines meeting at a right angle and separating is unambiguous at a glance, and always has been.

That makes routing a stated constraint on the writer rather than a matter of taste, so here it is in the form the code takes:

R1  Orthogonal only. Horizontal and vertical runs, right-angle corners. No diagonals, no curves. R2  Every vertical run owns a lane. The gap between two columns of boxes is divided into lanes; each edge crossing it takes one. R3  A lane is reused only when the two runs cannot touch — interval colouring on the y-spans, so a lane serves twice when the second run starts below where the first one ended. R4  A horizontal run sits at its own port's y. Two horizontals may share a y only if their x-spans are disjoint. R5  Two edges leaving the same jack share only the stub before they diverge, and the stubs are staggered so the split is visible. R6  A line never passes beneath a box. It routes to the gutter above or below and comes back. R7  Crossings are drawn plain — no hop arcs, no gap breaks. A second convention to learn is worse than a clean intersection.

R2 and R3 together are what a browser print could never have given, whatever the stylesheet said: the routing has to know about every other edge on the page before it can draw one.

Page 6 — landscape

Twenty-four cables, and you can follow every one

10

The orientation changes; the furniture does not

Same running head, same footer, same page numbering — the reader turns the sheet, not the document. fpdf takes orientation per page, so this costs one argument, and it is the only reason a section starts a fresh page anywhere here.

11

Eight edges, eight lanes — R2 doing its work

The eight drum sources fan into the stagebox across a 34 mm channel divided into eight 3 mm lanes. Every vertical run has the channel to itself, every horizontal run sits at its own jack's height, and the result reads as eight cables because it is eight cables. Drawn the obvious way — straight across, then down — six of them would have shared ink.

12

Under the multi, not through it — R6, and a legal crossing

Stagebox out 8 goes straight to the desk and the stage multi is in the way. It drops to the gutter below the box and crosses back up, passing over three lanes on the way. Each of those is a plain right-angle crossing: unambiguous, and left alone rather than decorated with a hop.

13

One jack, two cables — R5, and R3 above it

Lead vocal feeds the stagebox and splits to the stage multi. Both leave the same dot, so they share 4 mm of stub and then separate visibly. Meanwhile the three vocal runs reuse lanes 56, 59 and 62 from the drum fan forty millimetres higher — legal under R3, because the two runs on each lane cannot touch.

14

Paper metrics, and when it will not fit

52 mm box · 5 mm header · 4 mm port row · 6 pt labels · 3 mm footer. On screen those are 200 px / 18 px; box height is header + rows × rowHeight + footer in both places.

The graph is scaled to the text block and the scale is stated. If that scale would put a port label below 5 pt, it tiles instead — sheet 1 of N, cut on whitespace between boxes, never through one. An unreadable single page is not a smaller version of a readable one.

The decision the plan makes first

Three ways to get the drawing into Go, and why the roadmap's hardest one is the cheap one

The roadmap warned that only the node origin is in the database — box size, port rows, edge routing and colour all live in TypeScript the backend cannot call. That is true. What it could not know is that none of it is browser layout.

Chosen

Draw it in Go from the data

NODE_WIDTH = 200, PORT_ROW = h-[18px], SOURCE_ROW_HEIGHT = 20 — the canvas's geometry is constants and multiplication. The getBoundingClientRect() calls in InputGraphCanvas.tsx serve dragging, not a layout the browser computed. Go can do the same arithmetic in mm.

It does not have to match the canvas pixel for pixel to be right: this is a second rendering of one model, like the table. And the routing rules above have no counterpart on screen at all — the canvas draws one curve per cable in the cable's own colour, which paper cannot do. The export stays a plain GET, reproducible from a URL and testable without a browser.

Rejected

The client posts its geometry

Buys one layout implementation at the price of an export that cannot be produced from a URL, and it fails outright when the export is triggered from the Inputs tab, where the canvas is not mounted and there is no geometry to send. It also would not have solved routing, which is the part that actually needed writing.

Rejected

Split it — sizes derived, routing sent

Inherits the second option's mounting problem for the half it sends, and adds a seam between two halves of one drawing. Splits are where drift hides.

Every decision on one page

What this mockup commits to

D1

One document, one endpoint, five sections

GET /api/v1/events/{id}/audio-export/pdf returns Audio documentation — input patch, output patch, stage rig, signal flow inputs, signal flow outputs, in that order, in one file. Every audio surface offers the same Export PDF action and downloads the same document; there is one audio document and it does not matter where you asked for it.

D2

The chain column is deleted from both sheets

Its 186 mm go to the source columns on the input sheet and the destination columns on the output sheet. The path itself is sections 4 and 5's job, drawn rather than spelled.

D3

One table per sheet, with a continuation line for notes

The roadmap allowed a second table if one would not fit. It fits: nine columns in 186 mm on the input side, seven on the output side, seven on the rig. Notes and gap statements live on an optional second row rather than a tenth column.

D4

A stage rig section, between the tables and the diagrams

Six boxes with installed and patched jack counts, spare capacity by subtraction, and a total line. Every figure derived from data already stored. No location column — the application does not know where a box physically stands, and this document will not pretend it does.

D5

The flow is two sections, not one

Inputs on one landscape sheet, outputs on the next. They are two graphs in the code with two independent coordinate spaces, and a joint layout would have to be invented here and would exist nowhere else in the application.

D6

No two lines ever share a line

R1–R7 above, in full, as acceptance criteria. Crossings are legal and drawn plain; collinear overlap is a defect, and the only permitted case is the stub two cables share leaving one jack. This is the rule the section stands or falls on: every cable is the same black hairline, so overlap is indistinguishable from a single cable.

D7

Helvetica and cp1252 stay; every mark becomes a vector

No embedded font, and the binary stays the size the rental order left it. Arrowheads, port dots, phantom squares and colour stripes are drawn primitives — → is not representable in cp1252 and the diagram needed vectors regardless. Slice 41 inherits this decision.

D8

Colour is carried and never load-bearing

Channel colours reach paper as row stripes and port swatches. Every fact they express is also written — the document is complete in monochrome, which is what it will usually be printed in.

D9

The diagram fits or tiles, and states which

Scaled to the text block with the scale printed in the title line. Below a 5 pt label floor it becomes sheet 1 of N, cut between boxes, with a grid key.

D10

The phone gets the same export

MobilePanel's overflow item stops saying Print signal flow and calling window.print(); it becomes Export PDF and downloads the same file. §7h asked for every phone surface to reach the export it has, and a download is a download.

D11

Lighting stays on browser print, and §8 says so

LightingRigSheet.tsx was not in the request. Two mechanisms coexist afterwards, so design/patterns.md §8 — which currently describes browser print as though it were the only one — gains the rule for which surface uses which and why, and the routing rules gain a home. That amendment is why this needs approval before anything is written.

D12

The three converted React sheets are deleted

InputPatchSheet, OutputPatchSheet and ChainText lose their last caller. A zero-caller component is a second answer sitting where the first one goes. Their Vitest coverage moves to Go table tests against the generated document; PrintSheet and StagePlotSheet stay until Slice 41 takes them.

Not settled here

Two things the mockup cannot answer without a real event

The 5 pt tiling floor is a guess

Six boxes and twenty-four cables fit one landscape page comfortably. A 48-channel festival patch with eight boxes will not, and the point at which fitting becomes tiling has to be found against a real event rather than argued. The threshold is a constant in one place for exactly that reason.

Lanes are finite; very dense channels are not yet proven

R2 divides a channel into as many lanes as it has edges. If two boxes sit 8 mm apart with thirty cables between them, the lanes are a quarter of a millimetre and the rule defeats itself. The likely answer is that the fit scale accounts for lane demand, not just bounding box — but that is a number to find, and the tiling path is the fallback.
