stage plot measurements · revision 1 · for approval, before the spec

A plan you cannot measure off is a picture of a stage.

The plot already knows every distance on it — it stores centimetres and draws them at 1 SVG unit = 1 cm. What it will not do is say one. This slice lets you draw the distance you care about and leave it on the drawing, the way a drawing office does: two anchors, extension lines, a dimension line held off the geometry, arrowheads, and the number in a gap in its own line.

Three things make it a measured drawing rather than an annotation layer. The value is derived and never typed — the inspector states it and offers no field, because the moment a dimension can disagree with the geometry it is worse than none (P6). The anchors are features of the element's own box, so a corner survives a rotation, a resize and a rake, and is re-projected rather than re-found. And the same four dimensions print, on the sheet Slice 41 built, at the sheet's own weights.

The hard part is the one the request named: an edge anchor and the point anchors sitting on that same edge are the same feature of the same box, and you have to be able to tell which one you are about to grab. §02 is that, and it is live.

01 · the surface

Four dimensions, four different claims, one drawing

Everything below is drawn through projectElement and projectedOutline, ported unchanged, into a viewBox whose user unit is a centimetre. The riser is raked 14° in the plan, so its silhouette is a rotated rectangle and its faces are not axis-aligned — which is the case the perpendicular to a plane anchor exists for.

Top view · 1 sq = 25 cm. A · locked horizontal  ·  B · locked vertical, to a plane  ·  C · aligned, perpendicular to a raked face  ·  D · aligned, one free endpoint
A · horizontal
Speaker L centre → Speaker R centre, locked to the u axis. Both speakers sit at the same depth here, so the aligned value and the horizontal value agree — and that is exactly why the lock has to be stored rather than inferred. Move one speaker 30 cm upstage and this dimension must still read the PA's width.
B · vertical
The riser's upstage-left corner → the stage's upstage face, locked to the v axis — how far the riser stands off the back wall. The stage end is a plane: the dimension does not care where along that edge it lands, only that it meets it square. Slide the riser sideways and this dimension follows without ever being re-anchored.
C · aligned
The podium's west quadrant → the riser's stage-right face — the gap between them. The face is raked 14°, so the dimension is raked 14° with it, and the value is the perpendicular distance to that face's line. The foot is drawn where the perpendicular actually lands, and it carries a right-angle mark saying so.
D · free end
Wedge centre → a free point on the grid. The free end is a small × and it is anchored to nothing: it stays where it was put when the wedge moves, and it snaps to the plot's own grid when it is placed. This is how you dimension to a thing that is not on the plot — a socket, a trench, a mark on the floor.
02 · anchors · live

A dot is a place. A lit edge is a direction.

Arm the tool and aim at an element: it shows what it offers. Move the pointer over the riser below — the anchor you would grab lights, and the readout names it. Aim near a corner and the square wins; aim at the middle of a side and the triangle wins; aim anywhere else along that side and the whole side lights instead, with a ⟂ badge on the pointer.

That is the answer to the request's subtle half. A side's midpoint marker and that side's plane anchor are the same feature of the same box — one pinned to the middle, one free to slide. So they cannot be told apart by where they are, only by what shape lights up, which is P5 doing the work it was written for: shape carries the meaning, and the colour is the same indigo the canvas already uses for “this is what you will land on”.

Live. Pointer inside the frame · the aim radius is SNAP_THRESHOLD_PX = 8, the constant the drag snap already uses

The marker set is AutoCAD's, because half the people who will use this already know it

Nine anchors on a rectangle: a centre, four corners, four side midpoints — and four planes underneath the midpoints. They are not a list per shape. They are derived from projectedOutline, so a rectangle yields four of each, a raked truss yields six of each from its hexagonal silhouette, and an icon resource yields the corners of its own box. One derivation, no branch on kind — which is Constitution Principle II at the geometry level.

The ellipse is the one exception, and it is a fact about shape rather than a category. It has no straight side, so it offers a centre and the four quadrant points north / east / south / west, and no plane anchor at all: the promise a plane anchor makes — I don't care where along it, as long as we meet at 90° — is only keepable on a straight edge.

One side, three zones. Corner · midpoint · everything else — no dead ground, and a wrong pick is undone by moving three pixels
03 · drawing one

The palette gets its first mode, because you cannot drop a two-ended thing at a point

Every entry in StagePlotPalette today places an element at the viewport centre and selects it. A dimension has two ends and neither of them is the viewport centre, so the Measure section arms instead: the tile lights, the canvas takes a crosshair, and the bar states the mode, the next pick and the way out. M arms it from the keyboard, Esc leaves. A completed measurement leaves the tool armed, because a plot gets dimensioned in runs, not one at a time.

Three tiles rather than one tile and a modifier. Shift-to-constrain is a real idiom and it is deliberately not here: the mode is a stored property that the inspector can change afterwards, so it has to be nameable, and a thing you can name in the inspector should be pickable by the same name in the palette. One vocabulary, two places.

Two picks make a measurement — the offset is a separate gesture. A third click to set the offset is what CAD does, and it is what makes CAD's dimension command a three-step ritual you cannot leave half-done. Here the second pick commits: the dimension exists, at zero offset, running exactly between its anchors. Nudging it off the geometry is §04, and it is a drag with an undo.

At zero offset a locked dimension sits on the anchor you picked second. A horizontal dimension between two points at different depths has to choose a depth to sit at, and “the one you just pointed at” is the only answer that needs no constant. The first anchor grows the extension line.

04 · the offset · live

Moving the line is not changing the measurement

Drag the square handle on the left specimen. The dimension line slides off the geometry, the extension lines grow to reach it, and the value does not move — because the value is derived from the anchors and the offset is a placement. The handle is the canvas's own 8 px handle in #6366f1, the same square the resize corner uses, so a measurement's grab affordance looks like every other grab affordance on this canvas.

The offset is stored in centimetres and it snaps to the plot's grid. That is the one place in the whole dimension where a screen-pixel value would be wrong: an offset in pixels would slide the line across the drawing as you zoom, and it would have nothing to mean on paper. A negative offset puts the line on the other side, which is what the inspector's Flip does without arithmetic.

the geometry
Centimetres. The two anchors and the offset are positions on a measured drawing. They zoom with it and they print.
the lettering
Pixels, written n / zoom. Arrowheads, tick sizes, the extension gap and overshoot, the value type and the grab handle. This is not a new decision — it is the rule StagePlotCanvas.tsx already applies to every stroke it draws, and a dimension that broke it would be the only thing on the canvas that got illegible at 20 %.
on paper
Millimetres, fixed. An arrowhead is 1.8 mm at 1:20 and 1.8 mm at 1:500. Same rule, different unit of legibility: a dimension's furniture is sized to the reader, never to the drawing. Sizing the furniture in centimetres so it scales with the plot is the alternative, and it is what makes a 1:500 sheet's arrowheads invisible and a 1:20 sheet's arrowheads a foot long.
05 · anatomy

When the number will not fit between its own arrows, the number leaves

One rule, three lengths. 480 cm · 150 cm · 34 cm, at one zoom

One threshold, not two. CAD has separate rules for the text not fitting and the arrows not fitting, and the middle band — arrows in, text out — is where its dimensions look worst. Here there is one test, measured in pixels because both the text and the arrows are pixels: if the value plus its gaps will not fit between the arrowheads, the value and the arrowheads both go outside. A dimension is either wholly inside its own line or wholly outside it, and you can see which at a glance.

The value carries its unit. 1120 cm, not 1120 and not 11.2 m. The model is centimetres from end to end and a second unit is a second thing to reconcile; the unit rides at 0.8× in the muted ink so it never competes with the digits. Values display to the whole centimetre — the store keeps roundCm's 0.1 mm grain, and a dimension reading 1120.37 cm on a stage is noise pretending to be precision.

A dimension may carry a note of its own, above the value. Optional, empty by default, and on its own line so the number never has to share one. It is the only text on a dimension a person types, and it is the reason a sheet can say FOH trim rather than leaving a reader to work out why that particular 1120 was worth writing down.

06 · selected

Everything about a dimension is editable except the one thing it says

Palette · canvas · inspector rail — patterns.md §4, the stage plot's three columns, with the Measure section added at the head of the palette
value
Stated, never a field. It is derived from two anchors and a mode, so a field would be a place for the drawing and the number to disagree. P6, and the same reason the flow canvas has no cable-colour picker.
type
A select, not a segmented trough: Aligned · Horizontal · Vertical. A trough is for alternative views of one thing (patterns.md §4); this is a property's value, and the rail edits properties with selects. Changing it re-derives the value and re-places the line; nothing is re-picked.
from · to
Each end states what it is anchored to, on two lines — the element, then the feature: Riser / stage-right face, free point. There is no control here, because re-anchoring is a drag of that end's grip on the canvas, where the anchors light up as you pass them (§02). One gesture for picking an anchor, used the same way whether the dimension is new or twenty minutes old.
offset
A number in centimetres, and a Flip that negates it. The same value the handle drags, so the two controls cannot drift apart.
note
Optional short text, drawn above the value on screen and on paper. Empty by default.
layer
A select, like an element's. Which is the next section's argument.
delete
At the desk, and no confirm. This canvas confirms a delete that takes other things with it — a plot, a layer — and nothing else. A dimension takes nothing with it and is two picks to redraw at the machine that can draw it. Which is why the rail has this control and the phone's sheet does not: §09.

Selection reads the same as every other selection on this canvas

A selected dimension goes amber-500 — lines, arrowheads and value together — and grows three grips: the 8 px offset handle at its midpoint, and a grip at each end that re-picks that end by dragging. The dashed #6366f1 selection ring is for a box, and a dimension is not a box, so it does not get one; the hue change is the selection, with the grips as the second carrier (P4).

And a line is a hard thing to hit. A dimension paints at 1.5 px and carries a transparent hit stroke of 14 px on the desk and 44 px in the hand, as separate geometry from the paint. That is the port dot's rule verbatim — tokens.md §3, the paint and the target are different elements on purpose — and the 44 is amendment 1. Elements hit-test first, so a dimension lying across a riser never steals the riser's click.

07 · layers

A dimension takes its layer's switch and refuses its layer's colour

the call this mockup makes

Measurements live on layers — one layer_id, the same column an element has, inheriting visible and locked and printing under the layer key that already exists.

They do not take the layer's colour. Layer colour is how one piece of equipment is told from another. A dimension is not equipment — it is the drawing's own lettering, and it sits with the grid, the origin axis and the floor datum, which are not on layers' palette either.

The alternative — a separate annotation set with its own eye — was rejected on what it costs everywhere else: a second visibility switch on the panel, a second key on the sheet, and a print measurements? checkbox that nobody finds. The plot has exactly one mechanism for show me this and not that, and it is the one that already prints.

what the layer decides
Hidden. The layer's eye takes its dimensions with it.
Locked. Selectable and readable; the handle and the end grips are not rendered, exactly as an element's are not.
Ordering. Dimensions draw above every element regardless of layer order — a number under a riser is not a number.
Paper. A hidden layer's dimensions are absent from the sheet, for the same reason its elements are.
what it does not
Colour. One annotation ink at all times: zinc-500 for the lines, zinc-300 for the value. The number is the loud part and the line that carries it is quiet — P8, chrome recedes.
Which layer a cross-layer dimension belongs to. It is the active layer when it was drawn, and it is a select afterwards. A dimension from an audio speaker to a venue wall belongs to neither, and pretending otherwise would be a derivation with no ground truth.

One derived rule falls out of this and is worth stating as a requirement

A dimension is drawn only if its own layer is visible and every element it is anchored to is visible. Hide the audio layer and the speaker-to-wall dimension goes with it, even though the dimension lives on Venue — because it is measuring to something that is no longer on the drawing, and a dimension whose anchor you cannot see is a number floating in space. Free endpoints have nothing to hide behind, so a free-to-free dimension follows only its own layer.

08 · on paper

The object line is heavy and the dimension is light, which is how a drawing office reads

Sheet 1 of the document Slice 41 built, with §01's four dimensions on it at 1:100. The crossing rule in patterns.md §8 decides the ink without needing an exception: a dimension has no colour a planner chose, so it is a value nobody chose and it prints in the document's own ink rather than being crossed. What separates it from the elements is not hue — it is weight. Element outlines are 0.3 mm; a dimension line is 0.18 mm and its extension lines are 0.12 mm, the sheet's hairline.

A4 landscape · 1:100 · sheet 1 of 4. The title block's conventions list gains one row, which is the whole of what this slice adds to the document's furniture
per view
Sheet 1 carries the top view's dimensions, sheet 2 the front's, sheet 3 the side's. A measurement belongs to one view, so no sheet has to decide whether to show somebody else's.
furniture, not schedule
Dimensions get no row in the element schedule on sheet 4. A schedule answers what is it and how big about a thing on the floor; a dimension is a statement about the drawing, and it is already complete where it stands. What the title block gains is one conventions row and a count.
nothing meaningful is typed
Slice 40's rule, inherited: the arrowhead is a filled triangle and the free-end mark is two crossed strokes, both vectors. The document is built-in Helvetica through a cp1252 translator, so a glyph arrowhead would come out as a full stop.
the value is re-derived
The server computes the number from the stored anchors, in Go, from the same projection — it is not carried in the payload. A second rendering of one model, as §8a puts it, and the only arrangement in which the paper cannot quietly disagree with the screen.
09 · the phone

The phone reads every dimension on the plot, draws none and destroys none

patterns.md §7g settles the drawing without a new rule. Exactly one thing stays a desk job: direct manipulation of a spatial artifact — and picking two anchors on a scale drawing is that, twice, with an 8 px aim radius. So on a phone the tool does not exist, the palette's Measure section does not exist, and the offset handle and the end grips are not rendered.

What is drawn is drawn. Every dimension renders on the phone canvas at exactly the values the desk draws — 12 px value, 9 px arrowheads, the same n / zoom. Drawing is not targeting (P9), and a technician standing on the stage wanting to know how far the PA is apart is the best case for this feature, not an edge case.

Tap one and you get its sheet: both ends, the type, the value, and the note. Nothing else. Every geometry row is withheld — that is the spatial prop this file already has, applied to a new object — and the note is the one thing a dimension has that is not geometry, so the note is the one thing the sheet edits. It commits per field, which means this sheet has no footer and no Save, exactly as the element sheet beside it already works (patterns.md §7d) — and the safe-area inset sits under the last row of the body, which is the half of that rule the code had to be corrected on in Wave 6d (components.md, ui/Sheet).

And it does not delete. Not because deleting is a spatial gesture — it plainly is not — but because the phone cannot make one of these. A dimension removed in the hand can only be redrawn at a desk, so a thumb brushing a red control on a stage costs a trip to a computer to repair. Asymmetry of repair is the test, and it is general: a surface that can read an object but not author it does not get to destroy it. That is a different line from §7g's, and it is the one that puts this sheet where Slice 39 put trusses — read in the hand, authored at the desk — with a single editable field between them, which is the note.

The withheld gesture says so, once, where the editor would be

“Drawing dimensions is a desk job — open this plot on a computer. Tap any dimension to read it or to change its note.” A ui/InfoNote: open by default, closing to an i that reopens it, and the choice remembered. Not a banner, not a dialog, and not a greyed-out tool (P7).

10 · scope

What this deliberately does not do

Each of these is a decision taken, not a thing left out. They are here so the next reader finds a call rather than an oversight.

no angles
No angular dimensions, no radius, no diameter. The request is about relative position, and the plot's own rotations are already stated in degrees in the inspector and in the schedule. Angular dimensions are a second geometry, a second label placement rule and a second arrowhead; they are a slice of their own if they are ever wanted.
no chains
No continued or baseline dimension runs. CAD's continue chains a dimension off the last one's second anchor. It is genuinely useful and it is the first thing to add once the single dimension is in use — but it needs a rule about what happens when a link in the chain is deleted, and inventing that before anyone has drawn a chain is inventing a requirement.
no tolerance, no ticks
One arrowhead convention. Filled triangles, not architectural slashes and not a per-plot choice. A second convention to learn is worse than one you did not pick — patterns.md §8a's R7, on crossings, is the same argument.
no cross-view dimension
Already settled by the user, and worth its consequence being written down: a dimension drawn in the top view is not shown in the front or side view, even where its two anchors both project into them. Which also means a height is dimensioned in an elevation and nowhere else, which is where a height belongs.
no ellipse plane
An ellipse offers no edge anchor. §02. A perpendicular to a curve is a different perpendicular at every point, so “anywhere along it” is not a promise that can be kept.
no shift-constrain
The mode is picked by name, in the palette or in the inspector. §03. A modifier that produces a stored property the user then sees named differently is two vocabularies for one thing.
no delete in the hand
Decided by the user at review, and the reason travels further than this slice: the phone cannot draw a dimension, so it must not be able to destroy one — the repair is only available at a desk. The phone's sheet therefore ends at the note. Whether design/patterns.md §7g gains a clause saying that generally is a question this document puts to the reviewer, not one it answers; §09.
no auto-dimension
Nothing is measured for you. The request was “add measurements where I see fit”. A plot that dimensions every gap it can find is a plot you cannot read, and the judgement about which distance matters is the whole content of the feature.
